## Overview

Cerebras uses a strict industrial visual language. The page is driven by dark fields, white type, and a small orange accent that marks taxonomy, links, and emphasis. The mood is not polished consumer-tech; it is hardware-first, dense, and exact. Large areas of near-black and deep brown create the frame, while bright white content sheets break in as contrast when the site needs a readable list or form. That shift from dark stage to white sheet is one of the clearest structural cues in the system.

Typography is restrained and mostly mono-weightless. Manrope carries nearly all readable text, with a separate monospaced face reserved for tiny uppercase labels. The result is a voice that feels technical without looking coded or fussy. Geometry stays hard-edged, with 2px corners and minimal shadows. Orange appears as the only active color and works as a signal, not decoration.

## Colors

The palette is narrow and intentional. `canvas` is pure black and anchors the hero, footer, and many dark page bands. `surface-brown` adds a warmer, espresso-toned dark that supports the main marketing hero and the orange contact section. `surface-charcoal` is the cooler dark surface used for headers, filter bars, and other administrative page structures. These two darks are close enough to feel like one family, but different enough to mark a page change without introducing a new material.

`ink` is white and does most of the heavy lifting. Cerebras does not rely on soft grayscale text for hierarchy the way many sites do; instead, it keeps contrast high and uses weight, scale, and spacing to separate levels. The orange pair, `action` and `action-strong`, is the only visible brand accent. It appears in department tags, link text, button fills, and the faceted 3D contact artwork. The two orange values are close enough that they can be used as a single action family, with `action-strong` reserved for places that need a slightly hotter edge.

The visual relationship between colors is simple: black and brown hold the structure, white carries content, and orange marks motion or category. There is no pastel system, no multicolor product map, and no decorative gradient palette. That restraint keeps the page feeling serious even when the imagery is dramatic.

## Typography

Cerebras uses **Manrope** for the main interface and **Sometype Mono** for tiny labels. The supplied credit data does not support a designer or vendor attribution claim, so reuse should be checked separately. The hierarchy depends on size and tone more than on a deep type family stack.

| token | family | size | weight | leading | tracking | use |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| hero-display | Manrope | 2.25rem | 400 | 1.1 | -0.02em | Hero statements and large page leads |
| section-display | Manrope | 2.25rem | 400 | 1.1 | -0.02em | Section headers on dark surfaces |
| card-title | Manrope | 1.25rem | 500 | 1.3 | -0.02em | Job titles and feature headings |
| body | Manrope | 1rem | 400 | 1.5 | 0em | Descriptions, explanatory copy, and list metadata |
| body-medium | Manrope | 1rem | 500 | 1.5 | 0em | Buttons, emphasized links, and key line items |
| mono-label | Sometype Mono | 0.8125rem | 600 | 1 | 0.02em | Department tags and small taxonomy markers |

The major display style is quiet rather than theatrical. It uses a relatively small headline size compared with many AI brands, which makes the page feel measured and institutional. The body text remains very legible at 16px with a comfortable 24px rhythm. Manrope’s letterforms stay open, so dense dark layouts do not collapse into visual noise. The monospaced label style adds a machine-readable note without taking over the page. That label treatment is important: it signals category, not slogan.

## Layout

The page structure is built from full-width bands with strong contrasts between them. The hero places copy on one side and a large technical image on the other, often leaving a wide amount of dark negative space around both. This creates a long, calm reading path and lets the hardware imagery dominate without crowding the text. The hero can also include a small comparison strip or supporting metric panel, but it still behaves as a single-stage composition rather than a card grid.

The jobs section changes mode completely. A charcoal top bar holds the page title and filters, then a white inset sheet floats below it with a large margin on both sides. That white sheet is the strongest structural device in the system. It gives the careers content a document-like quality and turns the list into something closer to an internal index than a marketing block. Rows are generous, lightly separated, and left-aligned. The department tags are small and orange, which makes them easy to scan without overpowering the titles.

The careers accordion section adds a second split layout. Copy and the accordion list remain in the dark left half, while photography occupies the right half at full height. The image is not a background texture; it is a literal partner to the text. The same pattern appears in the orange contact section, where a text column sits against a sculptural orange field. These split sections are crucial because they turn the page into a sequence of controlled oppositions: dark versus light, text versus image, document versus display.

The footer closes the page with a very low-contrast dark band. Navigation is broken into compact columns with a small logo, muted utility text, and a legal line at the bottom edge. The footer is not dense in the same way as the jobs list. It is calm, organized, and intentionally reduced.

## Visual language

Cerebras leans on a few repeated visual habits. First, it treats darkness as structure. Large black and brown areas are not empty; they are the container that makes white copy, orange marks, and technical photography feel precise. Second, it uses industrial image content: wafer surfaces, data-center scenes, product rooms, and people working around hardware. The imagery is rarely soft or lifestyle-driven. Even portrait photography feels tied to equipment, awards, and engineering labor.

Third, the system prefers hard geometry. Corners are essentially square, panels are rectangular, and there is very little ornament. The visual interest comes from contrast, not from shape variety. The orange contact art is the exception, but even there the forms feel cut and faceted rather than playful. Fourth, the page keeps hierarchy readable through spacing and scale. Section headers have room to breathe, list rows are evenly separated, and the narrow labels stay tiny enough to function as indexing marks.

The overall result is severe but not cold. The warm brown background and orange accents keep the page from feeling sterile, while the white surfaces and clean typography keep it usable.

## Components

### Hero

The hero uses a wide, dark canvas with a large statement on the left and a high-impact technical image on the right. The most visible treatment is the combination of white text with a single orange emphasis inside the headline. That orange phrase acts like a highlight, not a separate brand color. Supporting copy sits below in a smaller Manrope style, followed by a compact white button. The button is rectangular, sharp-cornered, and visually flat. It reads like an action chip rather than a soft SaaS pill.

### Jobs sheet

The jobs page uses a strong document metaphor. A charcoal header band contains the title and compact filter controls, then a white sheet below holds the list. The sheet has clear left padding and a lot of vertical breathing room. Job groups are marked by tiny orange uppercase labels in Sometype Mono, followed by black titles and muted gray location lines. The right side of each row carries a small square or chevron-like affordance. This component should stay quiet and orderly; the list itself is the content.

### Accordion careers section

The accordion section splits the surface into two halves. On the left, the list is dark, with thin row separators and a visible active state that expands to show supporting copy and an orange link. On the right, full-bleed photography fills the available height. The orange plus/minus controls are tiny but important; they keep the interaction visible without introducing larger chrome. This component works because the list and image balance each other. Neither should become decorative scaffolding for the other.

### Contact panel

The contact section uses a maroon-brown field with large orange geometric forms. Copy is white and aligned in a narrow left column. Orange links sit under each prompt and act as the only interactive text color in the panel. The forms are not realistic product objects; they are abstract blocks that communicate energy, heat, and technical ambition. This component should remain bold and spare. If the orange art gets softer or more gradient-heavy, the section loses its edge.

### Footer

The footer is compact and system-like. A small brand mark sits near the top, followed by grouped navigation columns and quiet metadata at the bottom. Text stays white or gray on a very dark surface. There is no large promotional block here, no large card, and no heavy imagery. The footer’s job is to close the page with a disciplined register, not to extend the hero.

## Responsive behavior

On narrower screens, the page should keep its banded order: lead statement first, supporting action second, then the technical image or list. The white jobs sheet should remain readable as a single-column document rather than collapsing into cards. Split sections should stack in a way that preserves the text/image relationship, with copy staying above or beside the most important visual. The orange accents should remain small and purposeful at all widths. The same 2px corner language should persist; small screens should not introduce softer rounding just because there is less space.

## Practical implementation guidance

### Preserve

Keep the palette tight: black, deep brown, charcoal, white, and orange only. Preserve the white-sheet-versus-dark-stage contrast, because it is one of the clearest wayfinding cues. Keep the typography quiet and mostly single-family. Let spacing, not ornament, do the hierarchy work. Retain the tiny monospaced labels for taxonomy and the small rectangular action styles for buttons and controls.

### Avoid

Avoid rounded SaaS UI shapes, glossy shadows, pastel accents, and decorative gradients that are not already part of the orange contact art. Avoid over-using orange; it should mark category and action, not every callout. Avoid mixing multiple headline treatments or adding extra font families. Avoid turning the jobs list into cards, because the floating sheet is the point. Avoid making the footer loud; it should recede.

### Recommended build order

1. Set the dark palette and white text pair.
2. Build the Manrope hierarchy and the monospaced label style.
3. Recreate the hero with its orange-emphasis headline and sharp button.
4. Build the charcoal header and white jobs sheet.
5. Add the dark accordion section with the right-side image.
6. Add the orange contact panel.
7. Finish with the subdued footer and spacing cleanup.

### Accessibility

Keep text contrast high on every dark surface. Make the orange action color decorative only where it would still remain legible against black or white. Ensure the filter controls, accordion toggles, and list affordances have visible focus states that match the sharp geometry of the page. Maintain readable line length in the hero and contact panel. The white jobs sheet should not rely on color alone to separate groups; the tiny uppercase tags, spacing, and row structure all need to do part of the work.

## Scope note
