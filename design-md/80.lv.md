## Overview

80.lv reads as a calm editorial network rather than a loud magazine or a product landing page. The page uses a soft off-white canvas, white card surfaces, and a restrained olive-black text color so that the artwork inside each story card carries the energy. The interface never competes with the thumbnails; it frames them.

The hierarchy is built from two things: compact Euclid Circular B typography and rounded, card-based composition. The top of the page uses a horizontal story rail with tall cards and dark caption fades. Below that, the layout shifts into a denser editorial grid with a featured story column, stacked image modules, a small sponsored tile, and a trending list. The design depends on that change in density. Open space at the top, tighter information packing below.

The strongest visual signal is restraint. The site keeps chrome pale, borders soft, and text dark but not stark. The only clearly branded fill in the visible views is the pale sage action pill. Everything else stays quiet so the full-color artwork can lead.

## Colors

The page palette is narrow and purposeful. The base surface is a warm off-white, not a pure white field. White cards sit on top of that canvas and use very light borders or shadow to stay legible without looking heavy. Body copy is an olive charcoal, which softens the contrast compared with a colder black and gives the whole page a slightly natural, editorial tone. Near-black appears in the logotype, icon marks, and a few utility details where the design needs a harder edge. Muted gray-green text handles secondary metadata and sidebar labels.

| token | value | role |
|---|---|---|
| `action` | `#A1A490` | Sage-filled primary pill and similar quiet calls to action |
| `action-soft` | `#ECEEE4` | Light action fill and chip-like emphasis |
| `ink` | `#474B2F` | Main article text and headlines on white surfaces |
| `ink-strong` | `#111113` | Logo, icon marks, and the sharpest UI text |
| `muted-ink` | `#9EA5AD` | Dates, labels, and low-priority metadata |
| `canvas` | `#F9FBF7` | Page background and open breathing room |
| `surface` | `#FFFFFF` | Card and banner surfaces |
| `surface-soft` | `#F1F2F2` | Subtle panel backing and understated dividers |
| `surface-muted` | `#F4F4F1` | Quiet chip or inset surface |
| `border` | `#ECEEE4` | Hairline card and shell edges |
| `border-strong` | `#C6C6C6` | Slightly firmer dividers and progress bars |

The page also relies on image color for contrast. The top story cards mix vivid reds, violet glow, grayscale portraiture, warm skin tones, and cyan accents inside the thumbnails themselves. That color stays inside the artwork rather than becoming a UI accent system. The result is a shell that feels patient while the content stays energetic.

Use the palette as a hierarchy, not as decoration. `canvas` and `surface` should dominate the page. `ink` should carry most text. `action` should remain rare enough to feel intentional. `border` and `surface-soft` should do the work of separation instead of introducing darker rules or extra shadows.

## Typography

The visible voice is Euclid Circular B. It handles the logo adjacency, navigation, section titles, article headlines, metadata, and small utility text with a consistent modern sans profile. Applesystem appears only as a fallback/system family in the supplied typography data, so the design still reads as Euclid-led. Font licensing is not supplied in the packet.

| token | family | size | weight | leading | tracking | use |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `hero-display` | Euclid Circular B | 2rem | 600 | 1.375 | -0.015em | Large story titles and the strongest featured headlines |
| `section-display` | Euclid Circular B | 1.9375rem | 600 | 1.36 | -0.01em | Section headers such as “Top stories” and “Latest articles” |
| `card-heading` | Euclid Circular B | 1.125rem | 600 | 1.333 | 0em | Lower-grid article titles and compact editorial cards |
| `body` | Euclid Circular B | 0.9375rem | 400 | 1.333 | 0em | Metadata, descriptions, and supporting copy |
| `body-medium` | Euclid Circular B | 0.9375rem | 600 | 1.333 | 0em | Primary pill text and emphasized inline labels |
| `label` | Euclid Circular B | 0.875rem | 400 | 1.286 | 0em | Small tags, count markers, and sidebar labels |
| `navigation` | Euclid Circular B | 0.9375rem | 400 | 1.333 | 0em | Top navigation and category links |
| `legal-copy` | Euclid Circular B | 0.8125rem | 400 | 1.538 | 0em | Cookie text, legal lines, and the quietest utility copy |

The hierarchy is compact and not theatrical. Large headings are only a little larger than the body rhythm, which keeps the page grounded in editorial reading rather than marketing display type. Weight does most of the work. Six-hundred weight is used for section labels and article titles, while body copy stays regular and the quietest labels remain unforced. The result is a site that feels confident without shouting.

Line length matters here because many modules combine text with strong images. Headlines should stay short and break cleanly at the card width. Supporting copy should stay close to the headline and never drift into long paragraph blocks. The design works because the type is concise and the cards are tall enough to support it.

## Layout

The page is organized as a white shell on an off-white field. The top shell contains a logo, category links, utility actions, and a pale sage pill. It uses a rounded bottom edge and a subtle shadow, so it feels like a card pinned to the top of the page rather than a hard navigation strip. Under that, a second row of category links stretches across the width with very little visual noise. The whole header acts as a framing device for the content below.

The first content band is a horizontal story rail. It uses large, tall cards with rounded corners, tightly cropped artwork, and caption text sitting over a dark fade at the bottom of each thumbnail. The rail gives the page a fast browse rhythm. It is meant for scanning. Card widths are consistent, the gaps are small, and the row reads like a strip of covers.

The second band switches to a more editorial grid. On the left, a featured article block uses a text-first composition with a large headline, short summary, author row, and bookmark icon. In the center, stacked image-led modules use wide thumbnails with topic chips pinned to the bottom of the image. On the right, a compact sponsored tile sits above a trending list inside a white rounded panel. This arrangement creates a deliberate tension between large reading space and dense utility information.

Spacing stays generous between major bands and compact inside individual modules. Outer whitespace gives the page its calm. Inner spacing keeps the cards readable. The strongest separations are not borders; they are changes in surface color, card radius, and content density. That is the core layout idea of the site.

## Visual language

80.lv leans on editorial clarity, not ornamental branding. Its language is built from rounded rectangles, soft shadows, and strong image crops. The chrome is pale enough to disappear, which lets the thumbnails feel like covers from a magazine shelf. Most modules use white or near-white surfaces, so the image grid feels grounded rather than floating.

The cards have a friendly, slightly cushioned shape. Corners are rounded but not playful. The design avoids sharp corners, heavy dividers, and aggressive contrast shifts. Even the cookie banner follows this rule: a large white rounded panel, a dark headline, a small paragraph, and a compact icon badge at the right edge. Nothing about it feels abrupt.

The image treatment matters as much as the typography. The top row uses dramatic thumbnail art with dark bottom overlays for legibility. The lower grid uses larger editorial images, including a landscape-like image card, a character image, and a sponsored tile that behaves like a smaller billboard. The design depends on that mix of practical information and art-led visual weight.

Small utility markers are intentionally quiet. Bookmarks, numbered list markers, chip tags, and side labels sit in the background role. They support scanning without breaking the reading flow. That is why the page feels controlled even when the content itself is highly varied.

## Components

### Top navigation shell

- **Anatomy:** Logo, two rows of category links, a pale sage pill, utility links, and small icon marks.
- **Surface:** White with a soft shadow and rounded lower corners.
- **Typography:** `navigation` for the main links and `body-medium` for the pill.
- **Shape:** Rounded shell corners and a compact pill with the calmest possible emphasis.
- **Composition:** The top row feels lighter and more utility-driven; the second row expands the editorial categories across the page.

### Top stories rail card

- **Anatomy:** Tall thumbnail, dark bottom caption fade, headline, and short supporting text.
- **Surface:** Image-first, with the caption sitting directly on the card rather than in a separate block.
- **Typography:** Dark card text in the shell, then white caption text only where the bottom fade sits behind it.
- **Shape:** Tall rounded rectangle, consistent card width, tight lateral spacing.
- **Visible state:** The cards read as a horizontal shelf, not as separate isolated posters.

### Featured article block

- **Anatomy:** Section label, large headline, short summary, author row, date, and bookmark icon.
- **Surface:** White card surface on the canvas.
- **Typography:** `section-display` for the headline, `body` for the summary, `label` for metadata.
- **Spacing:** Plenty of vertical air between the heading, summary, and author row.
- **Hierarchy:** Text leads first; the image column nearby supplies the visual counterweight.

### Image stack modules

- **Anatomy:** Large image card, topic chips along the bottom edge, and another image block below.
- **Surface:** Full-color art with soft rounded clipping.
- **Typography:** Small chip text in compact, quiet type.
- **Composition:** The image fills the module; the chips sit low and do not interrupt the artwork.
- **Role:** These cards keep the page energetic while preserving the editorial grid.

### Trending list and sponsored tile

- **Anatomy:** Small ad card above, numbered trending list below, header label at the top.
- **Surface:** White rounded panels with subtle separation between list rows.
- **Typography:** `card-heading` or slightly smaller bold text for list items; `label` for the section header and counts.
- **Hierarchy:** The ad sits as a compact block; the trending list becomes the smallest reading unit on the page.
- **Visible states:** Rows are crisp and stack cleanly without heavy borders.

### Cookie consent banner

- **Anatomy:** Large heading, one line of explanatory copy, underlined text link, and a dark circular icon on the right.
- **Surface:** White rounded banner with generous padding.
- **Typography:** `card-heading` for the heading and `legal-copy` for the body line.
- **Shape:** Softly rounded, almost dialog-like, but visually calm.
- **Composition:** The icon acts as a right-side anchor and keeps the banner from feeling text-only.

## Responsive behavior

On narrower screens, the design should preserve the same reading order: header, story rail, featured article, image modules, then sidebar utilities. The story rail should reduce the number of visible cards before the cards become too narrow to keep their captions readable. The article grid should collapse so the featured text block remains legible above the sponsored and trending content instead of squeezing all columns at once.

The header should keep the logo and primary action visible first, then let secondary category links wrap or condense into a simpler arrangement. The pale sage pill should remain the clearest action surface, because it already behaves as a quiet focal point on the desktop layout. Image crops should stay stable; this design depends on recognizable artwork even when the layout tightens.

If the cookie banner appears on smaller widths, it should stack its text with the icon still visually separated, not compressed into a narrow strip. The whole system should continue to feel airy, with cards dropping to one column before the typography itself is forced to shrink too far.

## Practical implementation guidance

### Preserve

- Keep the page light. The off-white canvas and white card surfaces are central to the identity.
- Keep Euclid Circular B as the sole visible voice. The design depends on one sans family with a consistent editorial rhythm.
- Preserve the rounded card language and the soft, low-contrast shell edges.
- Let artwork supply the drama. The UI should frame the content, not compete with it.
- Keep the sage pill restrained. A quiet action surface fits the tone better than a saturated CTA.

### Avoid

- Avoid dark app-shell chrome or aggressive borders. They fight the airy editorial reading.
- Avoid adding extra accent colors to the interface. The thumbnails already provide plenty of chromatic variety.
- Avoid overly large headline scaling. This layout is confident because it stays compact and balanced.
- Avoid dense card shadows. The page reads through spacing and surface change, not through heavy depth.
- Avoid turning small utility items into primary visual events.

### Recommended build order

1. Build the header shell and keep the two-row navigation structure intact.
2. Establish the canvas, card surfaces, and border palette.
3. Recreate the top story rail with stable image crops and dark caption fades.
4. Build the featured article block and the image stack modules.
5. Add the sponsored tile, trending list, and the cookie banner.
6. Tune spacing, card radius, and type scale so the page stays editorial rather than promotional.

### Accessibility

- Keep the olive body text readable against the off-white canvas and white cards.
- Maintain enough contrast on the dark caption fades so the story titles stay clear over bright artwork.
- Give the small icon-only controls and bookmark marks obvious accessible names.
- Keep the cookie copy legible at a small size and avoid reducing the line height below the quiet rhythm already present.
- Use visible focus states that do not disrupt the calm shell; a restrained outline or inset ring fits this system better than a loud glow.

## Scope note

This guide covers the desktop home surface shown in the supplied views: the top navigation, story rail, editorial grid, sponsored tile, trending list, and cookie banner. It does not specify narrower-screen rearrangements, motion, hover states, loading states, or focus states. The type and spacing values are written in rem against the 16px base in the packet.
