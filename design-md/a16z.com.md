## Overview

a16z.com is an editorial home page with a single-minded hierarchy. The page opens with a burgundy brand bar, then hands nearly all attention to a giant gold medallion illustration set against a deep navy field. The central image is not decoration; it is the anchor that gives the page its tone. Text stays sparse and direct, with a short invitation and one primary action beneath the artwork. The lower section settles into a restrained footer that carries the logotype, a brief tagline, social links, and legal text.

The design feels formal, symbolic, and highly controlled. It does not rely on motion, busy panels, or a broad color range. Instead, it uses scale, placement, and a small set of repeated colors to create authority. Burgundy carries the brand and action state. White carries the most important text. The muted blue-gray tone keeps secondary information calm. The result is a page that reads like a statement rather than a content feed.

## Colors

The palette is intentionally narrow. `#061930` is the main canvas color and gives the page its nighttime depth. It is dark enough to let the gold artwork glow without needing extra effects. `#000000` appears as the deepest supporting surface and can act as an interior anchor where the page needs the strongest possible darkness. `#972641` is the action color and the most visible warm accent. It appears in the masthead and the call-to-action, so it should remain the color that means “act now.”

`#FFFFFF` is reserved for the highest-contrast text, the logo mark, and icon glyphs. It is the clearest voice on the page and should stay that way. `#8998AB` is the muted informational tone used for the footer tagline and other low-priority text. `#4B5058` can serve as a stronger subdued tone for supporting strokes or secondary labels when a darker gray-blue is needed. `#0000EE` is present as a traditional link blue, but it should remain a small accent rather than a competing brand color.

The relationship between these colors is simple: navy creates atmosphere, burgundy establishes the interactive signal, white handles clarity, and the gray-blue tones soften the lower page. The gold art is part of the illustration and should not be translated into a UI token. The page does not show a separate light-theme system; its identity depends on the dark field and the tight warm-cool contrast around it.

## Typography

The supplied packet points to one material family: **Proxima Nova**. Use it everywhere on this page. The hierarchy depends on weight, scale, and line rhythm more than on typographic variety. The headline is heavy and compact. The supporting line is large enough to hold attention but still secondary to the image. Footer copy stays in the same family and drops into a quieter color and size.

No family credit is supplied in the packet for this family, so no attribution claim should be added. Licensing should be confirmed before reuse. The design should not introduce a second family or a decorative fallback. Consistency is part of the page’s authority.

| token | family | size | weight | leading | tracking | use |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `hero-display` | Proxima Nova | 3rem | 700 | 1.31 | -0.03em | Main centered statement and large section headline |
| `supporting` | Proxima Nova | 1.25rem | 400 | 2 | 0em | Footer tagline and other calm support text |
| `body` | Proxima Nova | 1rem | 400 | 1.25 | 0em | Small links, legal lines, and general body copy |
| `body-medium` | Proxima Nova | 1rem | 500 | 1.25 | 0em | Navigation labels and quiet UI text |
| `button` | Proxima Nova | 1rem | 700 | 1 | -0.03em | Primary CTA label in all caps |
| `legal-copy` | Proxima Nova | 1rem | 400 | 1.25 | 0em | Footer legal row and low-priority notes |

The 48px headline is the strongest type size on the page and should stay tightly set so it feels emphatic without becoming cramped. The 20px supporting line can breathe more vertically because it sits below the image and does not need to fight for attention. The 16px UI text is the page’s workhorse size, used for links, labels, and footer content. Bold 16px text marks the primary action while keeping the label compact.

## Layout

The page is organized around one vertical axis. A thin top bar spans the full width, then the hero area takes over with a centered emblem that dominates the viewport. The image is the first large shape the viewer meets, and it should stay large enough that the page feels built around it. The headline and action sit below the emblem, centered and separated enough to keep the hierarchy clear.

Spacing is generous and measured. The content does not sit in a tight column or a dense card grid. Instead, it is placed in broad zones that let the top bar, the hero, and the footer each own their part of the page. The outer margins are wide enough to make the composition feel deliberate and editorial rather than utility-driven. This is important because the design depends on stillness and scale more than on a large number of modules.

The footer changes the rhythm without changing the language. It keeps the same dark field, but the layout splits into left and right groups: brand and tagline on one side, social links and legal text on the other. That split gives the lower page structure and closure. It reads as the end of the home page, not as the start of a new section family.

## Visual language

The visual language is ceremonial and symbolic. The gold medallion is the core image, and it carries the page’s emotional charge. Its circular form, engraved figure, and architectural detail make the page feel institutional and confident. The deep navy background gives the illustration room to glow, while the burgundy arcs in the field add rhythm without introducing clutter. Those arcs are subtle enough to support the center of the page instead of competing with it.

Flat chrome dominates the interface pieces. The top bar is a single color band. The action is a compact burgundy pill. The footer icons are small maroon pills with white glyphs. There are no heavy borders, no layered shadows, and no soft glass effects. The design works because it uses only a few shapes and lets them repeat. The circle of the hero image, the straight strip of the masthead, and the small rounded controls in the footer give the page a strong visual grammar.

The page’s tone comes from contrast with restraint. White is used sparingly so it stays strong. Burgundy appears in a few strategic places so it feels intentional. The muted blue-gray lowers the volume of secondary text. That narrow distribution keeps the page from drifting into a generic dark marketing style. It has presence because it is disciplined.

## Components

### Top bar

The top bar is a narrow burgundy strip running edge to edge. The Andreessen Horowitz logotype sits at the left in white, compact and upright, while the search and menu icons sit at the right with enough breathing room to feel intentional. The bar is brand chrome more than a navigation system. It should remain visually light and concise so the color can do the framing work.

### Hero stage

The hero stage is the signature component. It combines the deep navy ground, the centered gold medallion, and the subtle magenta arc pattern that radiates through the background. The illustration should remain the dominant shape in the viewport. The page’s short invitation sits beneath it in white, set large enough to read clearly but not large enough to fight the image. This stage works because the emblem is the center of gravity and the text only confirms the action.

### Primary action

The primary action is a compact burgundy pill with white all-caps text. Its size is intentionally modest relative to the hero image so it feels like a precise invitation, not a billboard. The same burgundy used in the masthead should carry the action so the page feels internally linked. The button should remain visually firm, with the rounded treatment matching the other small controls on the page.

### Footer

The footer uses the same dark field but lowers the intensity. The white logotype returns at the left, followed by the small muted tagline. On the right, the social links become a short row of compact burgundy pills with white glyphs, and the legal links sit beneath in small text. The footer is information-rich without becoming noisy. It closes the page with calm utility rather than with a new color system.

### Social tiles and utility links

The social tiles are small but important because they prove the page can carry secondary actions without losing tone. Their rounded shape keeps them aligned with the button treatment and the overall visual language. The legal links are small, low-contrast, and carefully subordinate to the brand mark and the main invitation. They should finish the page cleanly, not pull attention away from the center.

## Responsive behavior

On narrower screens, the same hierarchy should survive in compressed form. The top bar can keep the brand at left and collapse the right-side controls into a tighter group. The hero artwork should stay centered and prominent, but it may need to scale down before the text does so the emblem does not crowd the invitation. The headline should remain bold and readable, with the button directly beneath it.

The footer should stack before it becomes cramped. The brand, tagline, social row, and legal row can move into a vertical arrangement when horizontal space gets tight. Secondary text should stay legible against the dark ground. The burgundy action color and white text should remain the main contrast pair on every viewport so the page keeps its identity even when the layout compresses.

## Practical implementation guidance

### Preserve

- Keep one oversized hero object and one short centered invitation.
- Preserve the burgundy masthead and burgundy CTA as the main action color.
- Keep the typography family consistent across the page.
- Use the deep navy field as the primary canvas instead of adding light panels.
- Maintain the quiet footer rhythm with small social controls and subdued legal text.

### Avoid

- Avoid pastel palettes, card-grid layouts, and bright gradient treatments.
- Avoid heavy borders, large radii, or soft shadow systems that would weaken the hard-edged feel.
- Avoid stacking multiple CTA styles in the hero.
- Avoid mixing type families or adding decorative fonts for emphasis.
- Avoid filling the hero with extra copy; the page depends on spare wording.

### Recommended build order

1. Set the dark page canvas and the burgundy top bar.
2. Place the centered hero artwork and background pattern.
3. Add the main headline and the primary button beneath the image.
4. Build the footer with the logo, tagline, social links, and legal row.
5. Refine spacing so the vertical rhythm stays open at every width.

### Accessibility

- Keep white text against the deep navy and burgundy surfaces at a contrast level that stays legible under real device brightness.
- Give the search, menu, and social icons accessible labels.
- Provide descriptive alt text for the gold emblem so the hero still carries meaning when the image is unavailable.
- Make the button focus state obvious against the dark ground.
- Ensure the blue link color still reads clearly in the footer and does not sink into the navy background.

## Scope note

This guide covers the visible desktop home page: masthead, hero artwork, signup prompt, social row, and legal footer. It does not include mobile breakpoints, motion, hover or focus styling beyond the resting state, alternate article templates, or other unseen page families. Relative-unit values are rounded to the packet’s 2px step.
