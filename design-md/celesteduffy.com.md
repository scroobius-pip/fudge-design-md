## Overview

Celeste Duffy’s site is an art-first portfolio and shop wrapped in a restrained editorial shell. The visual language is built from two strong text voices, a paper-warm canvas, and a pair of saturated conversion colors that appear only when the page wants attention. Most of the page stays quiet so the illustration, print mockups, and framed artwork can carry the mood.

The system moves in chapters. Light pages use the cream canvas, thin black navigation, and large serif statements. Selling and contact sections shift into a deep dark field or a full coral band, which creates a hard contrast without adding extra ornament. The result feels personal and handcrafted, but the structure is still clean enough for a catalog, licensing inquiry, or contact flow.

The main traits to preserve are the paper background, the serif-led hierarchy, the sparse UI chrome, the framed artwork presentation, and the deliberate switch between light gallery space and bold inquiry or footer bands.

## Colors

Celeste Duffy uses a very small palette with clear roles. The canvas is warm rather than stark, the ink is a deep brown-black, and the loud colors are reserved for action surfaces instead of general decoration. That restraint keeps the artwork dominant.

| token | hex | use |
|---|---|---|
| `canvas` | `#F4F2EA` | Main page background, headers, gallery framing, and light form areas |
| `ink` | `#200603` | Headlines, navigation, body text, and labels on light or coral surfaces |
| `dark-surface` | `#000000` | Footer strip, dark contact band, and other heavy grounding sections |
| `action` | `#FE6B41` | Full-bleed inquiry band and the site’s loudest conversion surface |
| `accent-soft` | `#FEABDA` | Pink button fill, secondary CTA accent, and softer callout moments |

`canvas` and `ink` carry the everyday reading experience. `dark-surface` drops in as a dramatic anchor at the bottom of the page or inside contact sections, where it makes the serif type feel formal and grounded. `action` is the single high-chroma field color; it should stay rare so the orange band keeps its impact. `accent-soft` is gentler and works best on compact controls, where it softens the hard edge of the dark fields. The system does not need gradients or a broad tonal ladder; the power comes from switching whole surfaces, not from blending many shades together.

## Typography

Two families define the voice. **Libre Baskerville** supplies the editorial display layer, while **Almarai** handles the quieter UI and body copy. The serif is credited to Pablo Impallari Rodrigo Fuenzalida. Reuse should be checked against a valid license before shipping. The sans face is functional and plain, which lets the serif headlines and the artwork remain the focus.

| token | family | size | weight | leading | tracking | use |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `hero-display` | Libre Baskerville | 4rem | 400 | 1.06 | -0.02em | Large homepage statements and page-intro headlines |
| `section-display` | Libre Baskerville | 2.8rem | 400 | 1.11 | -0.02em | Big pitch lines in full-width inquiry bands |
| `card-heading` | Libre Baskerville | 2.2rem | 400 | 1.14 | -0.02em | Product titles, strong page subheads, and card captions |
| `control-label` | Libre Baskerville | 1.6rem | 400 | 1.2 | -0.02em | Prominent labels and form prompts that need serif emphasis |
| `body` | Almarai | 1rem | 400 | 1.5 | 0em | Paragraph copy, notes, and explanatory text |
| `supporting` | Almarai | 0.9rem | 400 | 1.5 | 0em | Smaller helper text, field hints, and footer detail |
| `navigation` | Almarai | 1rem | 400 | 1 | 0em | Upper navigation, utility text, and small chrome |
| `button` | Almarai | 1rem | 400 | 1.2 | 0em | Submit buttons and compact control text |

The hierarchy depends on size, contrast, and spacing more than on weight variation. Libre Baskerville stays regular and elegant rather than bold; it still feels large because the leading is tight and the letter spacing is slightly negative. Almarai sits flatter and more neutral. In the site’s best moments, the serif headline is almost poster-like while the sans text behaves like annotation under a framed print.

## Layout

The page is organized as a sequence of broad horizontal bands with generous internal padding. The header sits on the cream canvas and uses wide side breathing room, a centered logo, and a compact navigation row. That top area feels like a gallery label rather than a software toolbar. The layout then opens into large content fields where artwork, project descriptions, and forms take over the width.

The gallery and portfolio pages use a loose grid with framed pieces separated by clear gutters and pale negative space. Artwork tiles often sit inside a white or light mat, which gives each piece a printed, curated quality. The composition is not dense; it leaves air around every frame so the work can be scanned as individual objects rather than as a wall of thumbnails.

The commission and contact sections are the most structured parts of the site. On the coral band, the page splits into a left text column and a right form column. The left side carries one large serif pitch and a short explanatory paragraph. The right side stacks fields in tidy rows with consistent spacing between labels, inputs, checkboxes, and the submit control. The dark contact version follows the same logic, but the field surfaces become darker and the button treatment shifts to fit the deeper background.

The footer is a final full-width block. It uses the darkest surface on the page, larger serif headings, and a lighter body row for newsletter or site identity content. The footer is not crowded with borders; it depends on spacing and the strong surface change to mark the end of the page. Overall, the layout prefers broad sections, simple columns, and large vertical pauses over nested containers and busy separators.

## Visual language

The site’s visual language is quiet, handmade, and gallery-like. The strongest signal is the combination of cream paper backgrounds and black serif typography. That pairing gives the site a printed, archival feeling that suits illustration, prints, and licensing work. The logo mark is handwritten and loose, but the rest of the interface is disciplined, so the page avoids feeling craft-heavy or decorative for its own sake.

Artwork is presented as a set of framed objects. Some pieces appear as clean grid tiles; others appear in wooden frames or in product-style mockups. This framing treatment is important because it turns images into displayed work rather than casual thumbnails. Even when the page becomes commercial, the art still reads as the primary content.

Color is used as a chapter marker. The cream and black world handles browsing and reading. The coral band introduces the inquiry form with a louder and warmer tone. The dark footer and the dark contact section add weight and closure. Pink appears only as a small counter-accent, so it does not compete with the coral block. That limited palette keeps the site calm even when the page includes many artworks.

The linework of the UI is minimal. Underlines, thin rules, and small field borders do the structural work. Rounded corners are present but modest, which keeps the shell from looking soft or app-like. The overall feel is closer to a printed lookbook with a contact panel than to a modern SaaS interface.

## Components

### Header and navigation

The header uses a cream field, a centered script logo, small utility icons on the left, and a cart counter on the right. The navigation row sits below the logo and stays compact and uppercase. One item may appear underlined when active, which gives the page a quiet location cue without adding a louder tab system. The header should stay visually light; it frames the site rather than competing with the artwork below.

### Hero and gallery intro

The homepage hero and portfolio intro rely on a large serif statement and a generous open field. The heading sits centrally with ample room above and below, which makes the page feel spacious and curated. Under the statement, the grid of artwork tiles becomes the main event. The images vary in subject matter and framing, but they stay aligned by a calm grid and consistent gutters. This is the core browsing component of the site: one large sentence, then a wall of work.

### Print cards

The print-shop cards use a warm orange base with a pale image mat above the title area. The artwork or product image occupies most of the card, while the title and price sit below it in a serif voice. The card edge is clean and flat; the interest comes from the image itself and from the bright contrast between the orange card base and the cream artwork field. This component feels closer to a printed catalog spread than to a typical ecommerce tile.

### Commission and inquiry form

The full-bleed coral band is the most assertive component. On the left, a large serif pitch introduces the form and sets the tone. On the right, the form uses stacked labels, paired name fields, a wider email and phone row, checkbox groups, a date field, a large message area, and a submit button. The field fills are light and soft against the coral ground, which keeps the form readable without making it look clinical. The spacing between label groups is generous enough that each input reads as a discrete step in the request process.

### Dark contact panel

The dark contact version keeps the same overall structure but changes the emotional temperature. A portrait sits on one side, while the form occupies the other. The background is a deep brown-black rather than a neutral gray, and the fields glow more softly against it. The submit control is outlined or lightly accented instead of simply filled, which helps it remain visible without becoming loud. This component works best when the image and form stay balanced; neither side should shrink too far.

### Footer

The footer is a strong closing band with a dark ground, serif heading text, and a simple newsletter or identity cluster. The CTA area is not elaborate. It depends on one short prompt, one input, and one button, which matches the rest of the site’s preference for direct action. The footer should remain low in chrome and high in contrast, serving as a final anchor rather than a second hero.

## Responsive behavior

On narrower screens, the hierarchy should remain the same: logo and navigation first, then the large statement, then the gallery or form content. The two-column form layout should collapse cleanly into a single column without losing the label spacing or the strong section contrast. The artwork grid should reflow into fewer columns while keeping the framed feeling intact. The serif headings should scale down, but they should still feel substantial relative to the body text. Utility icons, counters, and small labels need enough space to remain readable, even when they become compact.

## Practical implementation guidance

### Preserve

- Keep the paper canvas warm and slightly creamy rather than stark white.
- Keep Libre Baskerville for the big editorial voice and Almarai for the quieter interface voice.
- Use the orange band only when the page wants a direct response.
- Keep the artwork framed and spaced so it reads as a curated wall.
- Keep the footer dark and simple so the page ends with weight.

### Avoid

- Avoid adding more accent colors; the site already has enough contrast with cream, dark brown-black, coral, and pink.
- Avoid heavy shadows, glass effects, and glossy gradients.
- Avoid turning small labels into bold UI badges.
- Avoid crowding the gallery with tight gutters or extra borders.
- Avoid replacing the serif display hierarchy with a generic sans system.

### Recommended build order

1. Establish the cream canvas, ink tone, and dark footer surface.
2. Set the Libre Baskerville headline scale and the Almarai body scale.
3. Build the header with the centered script logo and compact navigation.
4. Recreate the artwork grid and print-card presentation.
5. Build the coral inquiry band and the dark contact variant.
6. Finish with the footer and small utility controls.
7. Tighten responsive stacking and the spacing between form groups.

### Accessibility

- Keep the serif headlines readable against cream, coral, and dark surfaces; contrast should stay strong in every section.
- Use visible field labels, not placeholder-only form inputs.
- Preserve clear focus styles on navigation links, form fields, and buttons.
- Give gallery images useful alt text that names the artwork or product context.
- Make sure the pink button and coral band are never the only cues for an action; shape, text, and spacing should also carry meaning.

## Scope note

This guide covers the cream header, homepage gallery, print cards, coral inquiry band, dark contact panel, and footer on celesteduffy.com. Mobile stacking details, motion, hover styling, and alternate interaction states are not included.
