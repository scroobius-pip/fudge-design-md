## Overview

205.tf works like a type specimen bench: austere, exact, and dominated by the letterforms themselves. The interface does not try to soften the foundry experience. It gives the typefaces a pale stage, black rules, and enough open space for the specimen to carry the page. The result feels closer to a working archive or proofing table than to a marketing site.

The hierarchy is straightforward. Tiny utility text sits at the edges; a huge specimen letter owns the left side of the exposure page; the glyph matrix stretches across the rest of the width; and a single red action button interrupts the field only when the page needs a buying decision. The about page keeps the same discipline but shifts from specimen viewing to editorial explanation, using a left index rail and a dense text column. Across both views, the design depends on contrast between massive black type and pale surroundings.

What should stay consistent:

- A light gray field with white inset panels.
- Black specimen type and black hairline rules.
- Small, compact UI text in the same restrained face.
- One saturated red action, used sparingly.
- Grid logic that feels tabulated rather than card-based.
- Almost no decorative softness, shadow, or flourish.

## Colors

### Core palette

| token | value | role |
|---|---|---|
| `action` | `#FF0000` | Floating buy/try action and the only hot accent |
| `ink` | `#000000` | Specimen letters, rules, and primary UI text |
| `muted-ink` | `#9F9F9F` | Metrics, helper labels, and secondary metadata |
| `canvas` | `#EAEAEC` | Main page field and the background behind the workbench |
| `paper` | `#FFFFFF` | Inset panels, footer blocks, and reading surfaces |
| `rule` | `#000000` | Hairline borders and grid lines |

The palette is almost binary: black against off-white with one red interruption. That keeps the page feeling precise rather than decorative. The pale gray canvas carries most of the layout, while white panels carve out reading zones and specimen zones inside it. Black appears everywhere the site needs definition: glyphs, dividers, labels, and the grid itself. Red is reserved for the purchase decision, so it retains force even when the grid is dense.

The design does not rely on shadow or gradient to separate layers. Separation comes from surface color and the thinness of the rules. White surfaces are used for the main specimen panel and the footer structure, while the gray canvas is the ambient background that lets those white zones stand forward without turning into heavy cards. Keep that relationship intact. Any additional accent would weaken the single red interrupt and make the system feel less exact.

## Typography

205.tf uses two families only: **Exposure 100** for the specimen scale and **Mm 205** for the interface, labels, and long-form text. No extra families are needed because the identity comes from scale and contrast rather than from a mixed typographic system. The packet does not provide licensing details for either family.

| token | family | size | weight | leading | tracking | use |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `specimen-display` | Exposure 100 | 18rem | 400 | 1 | 0em | Giant glyph on the exposure page |
| `hero-display` | Exposure 100 | 3.1747rem | 400 | 1 | 0em | Smaller specimen headings and page-openers |
| `section-display` | Exposure 100 | 3.1747rem | 400 | 1 | 0em | Section marks and large editorial anchors |
| `body` | Mm 205 | 1.0417rem | 400 | 1.1 | 0em | About-page prose, footer copy, and explanatory text |
| `ui-label` | Mm 205 | 0.75rem | 400 | 1 | 0em | Metric captions, navigation, and compact utilities |
| `navigation` | Mm 205 | 1.0417rem | 400 | 1 | 0em | Top links and short navigational rows |
| `legal-copy` | Mm 205 | 0.75rem | 400 | 1 | 0em | Sale terms, privacy text, and terse footer notes |

The size jumps are the point. The workbench moves from 12px labels to 16.67px prose and then to a 288px specimen letter without changing families. That keeps the system coherent while making the specimen feel physically separate from the chrome around it. The UI type is small, regular, and unforced; it should never compete with the glyphs. The large Exposure 100 setting needs dense black mass and tight vertical control, while the Mm 205 text works best when it stays compact and factual.

Use the body style for the long about-page paragraphs and footer language. Keep the label style for all the small references around the specimen, the top navigation, and the newsletter field. The line lengths are important: the page reads as disciplined because the text stays narrow enough to feel editorial and wide enough to remain usable. The second Exposure 100 role is useful for smaller titles and section marks so the type system can scale without introducing a third family.

## Layout

The page is organized as a left-to-right proofing bench. On the exposure view, a white specimen panel occupies the left side, with metric labels stacked vertically and a giant letter centered inside the field. To the right, the glyph matrix runs across a very wide area in evenly spaced cells. That matrix is the main structural fact of the page: a strict grid of square compartments, each ruled in black and populated by a single glyph. The experience is less like scrolling through content blocks and more like moving across a catalog table.

At the top, the navigation is a slim utility strip. The words sit close together and keep out of the way of the specimen. The header is not a banner; it is a thin orientation line that gives access to the rest of the foundry without taking attention from the type. Small utility icons at the far right reinforce that same compact, operational tone. That strip should remain narrow and calm so the grid below can carry the page.

The footer mirrors the same discipline in a multi-column layout. A left mark anchors the foundry identity, a newsletter field runs as a single-line input, and the supporting links are arranged in separate narrow columns. The footer does not become softer or more ornamental than the rest of the site; it simply lowers the information density and keeps the same rules visible. Its structure depends on enough horizontal room for separated columns and a clear rule line above it.

The about page keeps the same layout language but replaces the specimen grid with editorial reading. A left rail acts like an index of people and topics, while the right column carries stacked blocks of explanation separated by thin rules. The rhythm is measured and document-like. Headings, subheads, and paragraphs all feel aligned to a single typographic spine, which makes the page feel like a serious archive rather than a promotional biography. The left rail must stay visually lighter than the prose column so the reading path is unambiguous.

## Visual language

The system is built from three recurring visual devices: hairline rules, large type mass, and flat surfaces. Hairline rules organize every major field. They outline the specimen panel, divide the glyph grid, separate footer columns, and segment the about-page copy. Because the rules are so thin, they feel technical rather than decorative. They give the page structure without adding visual weight.

Large type mass is the other constant. Exposure 100 appears as a heavy black form with blunt edges and no ornament. It behaves like a specimen rather than like a headline. The site lets that form sit in generous white space so the letter can read at a distance. The same logic applies to the glyph matrix: each cell is a frame for a single character, and the overall pattern reads as a catalog of shape rather than as a dense paragraph of text.

The third device is restraint. There are no soft cards, no visible gradients, and no shadow language doing the work of structure. Even the red action is flat and rectangular. That makes the page feel engineered and exact. The system asks the viewer to focus on proportions, spacing, and the shapes of the glyphs themselves. The design language should therefore stay almost severe: square corners, clean edges, repeated cell sizes, and no ornamental extras that weaken the grid.

## Components

### Utility header

- **Anatomy:** Wordmark at far left, a row of compact navigation links, and small utility icons on the far right.
- **Typography:** `navigation`.
- **Surface:** Transparent over the pale field so the strip stays light and operational.
- **Composition:** Keep the header thin and horizontally stretched. It should orient the user, not compete with the specimen stage.
- **Visible state:** Active or hovered links should remain simple; the header works because it is quiet.

### Specimen panel

- **Anatomy:** White inset field, stacked metric labels, a centered giant letter, and thin horizontal guides.
- **Typography:** `specimen-display` for the letter, `ui-label` for the metric captions.
- **Shape and structure:** Square corners, strict alignment, and black rules that frame the measurement zones.
- **Composition:** The giant glyph should sit slightly off-center in a way that keeps the surrounding labels readable. The panel feels like a proofing instrument, not a hero card.
- **Hierarchy:** The specimen is the main subject. The metrics support it without becoming decorative.

### Glyph matrix

- **Anatomy:** Repeating square cells, section labels such as the Latin-1 block, and centered glyphs.
- **Typography:** `ui-label` for section labels; the glyphs themselves use the same Exposure 100 specimen scale as the panel.
- **Surface:** Pale field with black grid lines; the cells remain flat.
- **Spacing:** Even gutters and consistent cell dimensions are what make the matrix legible.
- **Composition:** Keep the cells even and the rules sharp so the matrix reads as a specimen table rather than as body copy.

### Floating action

- **Anatomy:** A small fixed red rectangle with short white text.
- **Typography:** `ui-label`.
- **Surface:** `action` background with white type.
- **Composition:** Keep it outside the main reading path so it feels like a decisive interrupt rather than a banner.
- **Hierarchy:** This is the only hot color in the system. Use it once, and let it stay memorable.

### About index and prose column

- **Anatomy:** Left-side list of names or topics, right-side explanatory blocks, and thin dividers between sections.
- **Typography:** `ui-label` for the index, `body` for the prose, with section labels allowed to feel slightly larger only when they need to anchor a block.
- **Surface:** White or pale panels against the gray canvas.
- **Composition:** Keep the left rail narrow and the text column wide enough to read as an essay.
- **Visible state:** The section separators should be plain rules. The page gains clarity from the spacing between blocks, not from decoration.

### Footer strip

- **Anatomy:** Foundry mark and short descriptor, newsletter field, social links, and legal column.
- **Typography:** `legal-copy` and `ui-label` for short labels and navigation, `body` for longer text.
- **Surface:** Canvas-backed strip with a thin top rule; reserve white inset panels for the specimen and reading areas.
- **Composition:** Arrange the footer as a balanced set of columns on the pale field rather than as a stacked marketing module.
- **Hierarchy:** The newsletter prompt should lead, but the legal links must remain easy to find.

## Responsive behavior

The page is built for wide desktop room. The specimen panel, the long glyph grid, the split about page, and the multi-column footer all depend on horizontal space to stay legible. When the width tightens, the main requirement is to preserve the same hierarchy: keep the specimen readable first, keep the grid cells even, and keep the footer columns from collapsing into an unreadable mass. The page should remain a workbench, not become a different visual language. Any narrower adaptation should protect the rules, labels, and the single red action before it changes anything else. Keep the grid and the footer in view as long as possible; they are core to the page identity.

## Practical implementation guidance

### Preserve

- Keep the page mostly monochrome so the glyphs remain the focus.
- Use Exposure 100 only for the specimen and major section marks.
- Use Mm 205 for all chrome, labels, footer copy, and long-form text.
- Keep the grid rules thin and black.
- Keep the red action isolated and rare.
- Let white panels and the gray canvas do the structural separation.
- Preserve square corners and flat fills so the system stays crisp.

### Avoid

- Avoid rounded cards, soft drop shadows, and decorative gradients.
- Avoid giving the header more visual weight than the specimen.
- Avoid mixing additional type families into the interface.
- Avoid centering every block; the layout needs left alignment and measured asymmetry.
- Avoid turning the footer into a marketing banner.
- Avoid extra accent colors that weaken the red action.
- Avoid softening the grid lines; the thin rule language is part of the identity.

### Recommended build order

1. Establish the gray canvas, white panels, black rules, and the red action.
2. Build the top utility header and the small type scale.
3. Recreate the specimen panel with the giant letter and metric guides.
4. Build the glyph matrix as a strict cell system.
5. Add the floating action in its fixed lower-right position.
6. Add the about-page index and prose layout.
7. Finish with the footer columns and newsletter line.

### Accessibility

- Keep contrast high wherever black type sits on white or gray surfaces.
- Preserve readable line lengths in the about page and footer.
- Give the grid a clear keyboard focus treatment if the cells are interactive.
- Make the red action readable without relying on color alone; the button shape and placement should carry meaning too.
- Keep labels large enough to remain legible at a distance, especially in the metric panel and the footer.
- Maintain the thin rules with enough contrast that they still separate sections for low-vision users.

## Scope note

This guide covers the desktop exposure page, the about page, and the footer structure shown in the supplied views. Narrow-screen rules, hover and focus states, glyph-cell selection states, motion, loading behavior, and alternate page families are not included here. The spacing scale is rounded to a 0.25rem step for implementation clarity.
