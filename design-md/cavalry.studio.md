## Overview

Cavalry’s homepage uses a compact, aggressive visual language built for a motion tool. The page is not neutral or airy. It is a dark stage with a vivid purple chapter color, crisp white containers, and a bright yellow call to action. That contrast gives the page a gallery feel without losing product clarity. The strongest sections read like poster panels: large headline, short supporting copy, and one visual anchor that explains the topic faster than a paragraph could.

The design depends on a steady alternation between open color fields and dense information blocks. Purple sections hold the hero, feature story, and FAQ. Black sections carry trust and footer material. White cards sit inside both dark fields as the main reading surface. The overall effect is disciplined rather than decorative. The page allows the artwork and examples inside the cards to bring in extra color, while the chrome around them stays tightly controlled.

## Colors

| token | value | use |
|---|---|---|
| `canvas` | `#000000` | Full-page dark base, footer, trust strip, and the quiet field behind the brightest white text |
| `surface` | `#6437FF` | Hero background, feature chapters, FAQ field, and the dominant brand color band |
| `action` | `#FFFF00` | Primary download buttons and the clearest decision points on the page |
| `ink` | `#FFFFFF` | Headlines, body copy on dark fields, icons, and link text on black or purple surfaces |
| `panel` | `#FFFFFF` | Feature cards, FAQ cards, download cards, and other bright containers that break up the dark page |

The palette is intentionally narrow. Black establishes depth and gives the brand room to breathe. Purple sets the page’s pace and makes each major section feel like a chapter break. White is the main reading plane and the main card color. Yellow is reserved for direct action so it lands as a signal, not a general accent. The examples and thumbnails inside the cards introduce extra hues, but those colors belong to the content inside the panels, not to the page chrome.

Light and dark do not compete as separate themes. They trade off by section. Black sections flatten the page into a crisp frame. Purple sections carry the active marketing narrative. White panels create the highest-contrast information blocks in both modes. That mix keeps the site energetic while keeping the interface readable.

## Typography

Canva Sans carries almost all of the page voice. It appears in the hero, section headings, card titles, body copy, navigation, and footer text. That consistency is what makes the page feel coherent even as the background color changes from section to section. Arial appears only in button labels, where a slightly more utilitarian tone helps the calls to action feel direct. Canva Sans is credited to Colophon Foundry. Arial has no supplied credit entry, so no attribution claim should be made for it.

| token | family | size | weight | leading | tracking | use |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `hero-display` | Canva Sans | 4rem | 400 | 1.1 | 0em | The opening statement and the largest product message |
| `section-display` | Canva Sans | 2rem | 600 | 1.25 | 0em | Chapter headings such as feature, trust, and FAQ leads |
| `card-heading` | Canva Sans | 1.5rem | 600 | 1.25 | 0em | Card titles, download labels, and compact content headers |
| `lead` | Canva Sans | 1.3125rem | 400 | 1.5 | 0em | Short explanation under the hero and in white panels |
| `body` | Canva Sans | 1rem | 400 | 1.5 | 0em | General descriptive copy, card text, and secondary detail |
| `nav` | Canva Sans | 1.125rem | 600 | 1.3333333333 | 0em | Header links and other high-contrast navigation labels |
| `button` | Arial | 1.125rem | 600 | 1.3333333333 | 0em | Filled and outlined buttons, especially the yellow CTA |
| `footer-copy` | Canva Sans | 0.875rem | 400 | 1.5714285714 | 0em | Footer links, legal text, and the smallest stable reading text |

The hierarchy is straightforward. Size and weight do the work; ornament is absent. The hero uses the largest size but stays regular-weight so it feels broad rather than heavy. Section headings shift to semibold so the page can move into a more structured mode once the viewer reaches the cards and detail sections. Body copy is compact, with generous leading for dark backgrounds. Button labels are heavier and slightly more compressed, which keeps them from blending into the surrounding copy. The result is a type system that feels clear on black, clean on purple, and calm inside white cards.

## Layout

The page is arranged as a sequence of broad chapters with strong color boundaries. The hero sits at the top on purple, with a left-aligned text column and floating example artwork to the right. That asymmetry gives the opening section energy without making it chaotic. The navigation stays slim and restrained so the hero remains the dominant event. The next major section shifts to a white feature panel inside the purple field, which makes the content feel more like a structured catalog than a dense marketing block.

Mid-page, the page uses repeated card geometry to keep long content readable. The learning section relies on a three-card row with white panels, rounded corners, vivid thumbnails, purple titles, and short descriptions. The download area uses a two-card split with clear contrast between the free offer and the enterprise offer. That split is important: the cards are related, but they should not blend together. One side can stay bright and open, while the other can invert into purple to create a stronger visual fork.

The trust section is much quieter. A black strip, a short heading, and a tight field of logos create a pause before the FAQ. The FAQ then returns to purple and uses a large white accordion card to hold a substantial amount of text without making the page feel heavy. The footer closes the page in black and compresses the utility content into a calm, centered band. Large section spacing and wide side gutters keep the page from feeling crowded even when the content becomes dense.

## Visual language

The visual language depends on contrast, measured rounding, and a poster-like use of space. Black and purple are the structural colors; white is the main container; yellow is the only color that should feel urgent. That restraint keeps the interface from becoming noisy, even though the examples and promotional images are full of bright motion-design color. The page chrome stays strict so the artwork can feel expressive.

Rounded rectangles are the main shape family. They are not soft enough to feel playful, and they are not sharp enough to feel technical. That balance is what gives the page its character. The cards feel like mounted prints or display boards. Buttons are pill-shaped, but the pill is short and firm rather than bubbly. Linework stays thin and crisp. Shadows are absent, so depth comes from spacing, section breaks, and the contrast between dark fields and bright panels.

The page also uses a subtle sense of order that comes from alignment. Section headings line up with card edges. Buttons sit where the eye expects them. The grid pattern in some purple areas is extremely quiet and acts more like texture than decoration. Overall, the page says “structured creative tool” rather than “generic software marketing.” It feels energetic, but it does not drift into glossy theatrics.

## Components

### Header
The header is a slim top bar with the Cavalry mark on the left, a small cluster of links, and a bright call to action on the right. It needs to stay visually light so it does not compete with the hero. The text should remain white on dark fields, and the CTA should keep the yellow fill so it reads as the clear route forward.

### Hero stage
The hero combines an oversized headline, a short supporting line, one or two action buttons, and a cluster of floating example visuals. The text block is compact and left-aligned. The imagery spreads outward and slightly downward, which creates motion before any animation begins. This section is about scale and contrast. The headline must feel large enough to anchor the whole page, and the supporting copy must stay secondary.

### Learning cards
The learning cards are white panels with rounded corners, a bright thumbnail banner at the top, a purple title, black body copy, and a small outline button. Their power comes from repetition. Each card uses the same interior rhythm, so the row reads as a coherent set. The images can be visually loud, but the cards themselves should stay clean, bright, and calm.

### Feature grid
The feature section places a large white panel inside a purple field and fills it with an icon-and-label grid. The list should feel easy to scan. The typography is heavier than the body copy used in other areas, which helps the feature names feel like concrete product capabilities rather than plain explanatory text. The spacing inside the panel should stay generous so the grid can breathe.

### Download split
The download area is built as a two-card fork. The free offer uses a white card with dark copy and a prominent button. The enterprise offer flips the field so the contrast is obvious at a glance. That inversion is the key visible idea. One side should look immediately open and direct; the other should feel more closed and sales-oriented. Both cards need the same rounded geometry so the split feels intentional rather than mismatched.

### Trust strip
The trust strip is a black section with a short heading and a centered cluster of brand marks. It should feel like a pause in the narrative, not a loud sales wall. The logos do most of the visual work, so the section text can stay concise. Keep the spacing wide and the composition centered to make the strip feel calm and established.

### FAQ accordion
The FAQ uses a large white accordion card on purple. One answer is open, which gives the section enough visual depth to feel substantial. The question rows should stay crisp and evenly separated. The open state must feel easy to read, with enough line length and vertical space for the answer text to hold together. The section depends on the contrast between the white card and the purple field around it.

### Footer
The footer is a black information band with the logo, social icons, a language selector, compact links, and legal text. It should feel complete without becoming busy. White text works best here, and the icon row should remain tight so the section reads as a clean endpoint. The footer is the quietest part of the page and should stay that way.

## Responsive behavior

On narrower screens, the reading order should stay intact before anything else changes. The hero should keep the headline first, then the action, then the artwork. The learning cards can stack from three columns into one or two columns, but their order should not change. The feature grid should wrap cleanly without reordering items. The download split should remain clearly two separate offers even when it becomes vertical. The FAQ should keep the open row legible and easy to tap. The footer should compress into stacked groups rather than shrinking text until it loses clarity.

## Practical implementation guidance

### Preserve
- Keep the black, purple, white, and yellow palette intact.
- Keep Canva Sans as the main face and Arial only for button labels.
- Preserve the large headline scale and the short-copy rhythm.
- Keep rounded white cards as the main container shape.
- Keep the yellow CTA as the clearest decision point on the page.

### Avoid
- Avoid adding extra brand colors to the page chrome.
- Avoid heavy shadows or glossy surfaces.
- Avoid turning every block into a small pill-shaped card.
- Avoid shrinking the hero into a generic software banner.
- Avoid making the footer feel airy; it should stay compact and grounded.

### Recommended build order
1. Set the color tokens and type scale.
2. Build the header and hero stage.
3. Add the learning cards and feature grid.
4. Build the download split with its two contrasting offers.
5. Add the FAQ card and accordion treatment.
6. Finish with the footer and legal band.
7. Compress the layout for smaller screens while keeping the same section order.

### Accessibility
- Keep white text large enough and paired with enough surrounding space on black and purple fields.
- Give the yellow buttons a clear focus state that stays visible on both dark and bright backgrounds.
- Make the accordion keyboard-friendly and keep the open state easy to understand.
- Provide useful alternative text for the learning thumbnails and any logo or brand images.
- Keep line lengths short enough that the hero, FAQ, and footer remain easy to scan.

## Scope note

This guide covers the desktop homepage and its visible sections: hero, learning cards, feature grid, download split, trust strip, FAQ, and footer. It does not include mobile breakpoints, motion behavior, hover or disabled states, or alternate product pages. Spacing and radii use practical rem values aligned to the interface scale.
