# How e360.yale.edu is designed

## Design character

Yale Environment 360 is a serious editorial magazine system: warm paper neutrals, coral-red accents, black display headlines, and large documentary photography. It feels academic yet public—long-form environmental journalism with a calm, print-influenced web layout.

## Foundations

### Color

Measured hits:

- **Accent coral:** `#ff6654` — category labels, key UI sparks, brand energy
- **Ink black:** `#000000` / `#1d1c1d` — headlines and primary text
- **Warm stone:** `#7c7363` — muted meta and secondary text
- **Paper backgrounds:** off-white / light warm gray (visual)
- **Photography:** full-color environmental imagery as primary emotion

Coral is used sparingly so photos and black type stay dominant.

### Typography

No declared family. Observed sizes:

- Small caps-like meta / labels ~10.5px (400–700)
- Body ~17px / ~23px line-height, weight 400
- Strong titles around 15–16px+ upward into large display headlines on heroes (visual: multi-line black display over imagery)
- Editorial serif/sans mix is suggested visually on magazine-style titles; family names unconfirmed

### Spacing and layout

- Centered content with large side margins (~202px) and 20px inner padding
- Deep section bottoms (~100px) and 10–12px micro spacing between stacked editorial items
- Feature stories: full-bleed or wide photo with overlaid or adjacent headline block
- Secondary modules as horizontal story rows and issue/archive lists

## Visual language

- Documentary photography first
- High-contrast black type on light paper
- Coral as a precise accent (not a fill color)
- Quiet rules and lots of breathing room between stories

## Components and states

- Masthead with Yale E360 wordmark
- Primary nav for magazine sections
- Hero feature with large image + headline
- Story cards / list rows with thumbnail, title, dek
- Category labels in accent color
- Footer with institutional links

## Responsive behavior

Both captures are wide desktop (1712×1314). Mobile reflow not observed.

## Practical guidance

**Preserve**

- Warm paper + black type + coral accent triad
- Photography-led feature hierarchy
- Generous vertical sectioning and centered reading measure

**Avoid**

- Startup-style gradients or glass cards
- Overusing coral as large fills
- Crowding multiple heroes above the fold
