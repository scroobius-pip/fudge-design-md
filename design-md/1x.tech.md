## Overview

1x.tech presents NEO as a premium home robot through restraint rather than spectacle. The page keeps the field quiet, lets the robot image occupy the largest shape, and uses small dark text, pale panels, and soft radii to make the product feel calm and engineered. The structure is chapter-like: a hero purchase screen, an accessories row, a technical specification area, modal-style feature detail, and a sparse footer. Each chapter changes density, but the visual language stays consistent.

The strongest characteristic is the contrast between a huge product image and a compact control rail. The hero section uses a broad pale field with a black sculptural robot and a narrow stack of pricing and purchase details. Lower sections reduce the image scale and increase information density: accessory cards become evenly spaced product showcases, the hardware section becomes a clean data grid, and the modal views mix soft cards with editorial photography. The system relies on spacing, alignment, and rounding more than on color variation.

## Colors

| token | value | role |
|---|---|---|
| `ink` | `#000000` | Ink |
| `action` | `#000000` | Action |
| `canvas` | `#F7F6F2` | Canvas |
| `surface` | `#EFEDE8` | Surface |
| `surface-strong` | `#E7E4DE` | Surface strong |
| `on-dark` | `#FFFFFF` | On dark |

The palette is intentionally narrow. Black text and dark controls carry almost all emphasis. The canvas is a warm white rather than a bright clinical white, which keeps the robot and the cards feeling soft instead of harsh. Pale cream and stone panels support product showcases, while a slightly stronger neutral appears in grouped pricing cards and dialog shells. Rules are thin and quiet; they separate content without turning the page into a wireframe. Shadows are minimal and diffuse, used only to lift cards or buttons just enough to show depth.

The page does not depend on a brand accent hue. Instead, hierarchy comes from value contrast: dark robot body, light field, mid-tone panel, and near-black button. The best interpretation is a monochrome system with warm neutrals. Interactive elements should stay dark and solid, while secondary surfaces should remain close to the canvas and avoid saturated tinting. In practice, this means the interface reads as premium and technical at the same time: serious enough for a hardware spec sheet, soft enough for a consumer product launch.

## Typography

Abc Diatype carries the entire page. The hero and section titles use bold, compact forms with tight tracking. Supporting text stays small and calm, with enough line height to keep the narrow control rail readable. Labels and legal copy are understated; they should never fight the product image or the technical tables. Abc Diatype is credited to Johannes Breyer and Fabian Harb at Dinamo. Font licensing is not specified.

| token | family | size | weight | leading | tracking | use |

The hierarchy depends on weight and spacing more than on dramatic size jumps. Headings are centered or left-aligned depending on context, but they remain compact. Body copy is short and often grouped into tight lists, which gives the page a technical tone without making it feel dense. The system works best when text is held to short lines and isolated with generous padding.

## Layout

The hero establishes the layout grammar: one dominant image block, one narrow information rail, and a lot of surrounding white space. On desktop, the robot image expands leftward and up to the edges of a rounded container, while the right rail stacks the title, two pill tabs, a row of swatches, two pricing cards, a deposit note, and a dark button. The composition is asymmetric but stable. The main image gets the emotional weight; the rail provides the transactional path.

The accessory section changes the rhythm from asymmetry to even repetition. Three same-height cards span the width of the page, each with a centered object and a short label near the bottom. Their shared size and pale fill create a gallery-like rhythm. The section label above them is tiny and widely tracked, which keeps the row feeling curated rather than promotional.

The hardware section trades image presence for density. It is arranged as a structured grid with thin dividers and paired columns of labels and values. The left side typically names a subsystem; the right side aligns the measurements or capabilities. The layout works because the white space is still controlled. Even with many entries, the page never collapses into a spreadsheet feel. There is enough breathing room between groups to preserve the same premium tone established in the hero.

The modal sections use a large rounded shell that sits above a dimmed scrim. Their internal grids are softer and more editorial than the hardware table. The utility modal balances short explanatory cards at the top with photographic tiles below and a smaller card row at the bottom. The FAQ modal, by contrast, becomes a stack of rounded question rows with even vertical spacing. Both versions keep the same outer shell, so the page can shift from product storytelling to support content without changing its base geometry.

The footer is nearly the opposite of the hero. It uses a wide, almost empty field, a tiny logo at the left, and several link columns set far apart. That large negative space is part of the composition, not missing content. It gives the page a quiet ending and makes the footer links feel secondary to the product itself.

## Visual language

The visual language is shaped by three repeated ideas: rounded containment, soft engineering, and spacious presentation. Rounded rectangles are everywhere, but they are not decorative. They frame the robot card, the pricing module, the accessory tiles, the modal shell, and the footer-like utility blocks. The radii are generous enough to soften the industrial subject matter, yet not so large that the page turns playful.

Photography and renderings are treated with the same discipline. The robot is always presented as a clean object against a light field. Utility imagery appears inside darkened photo tiles with captions sitting low in the frame. This makes the photography functional: it explains use cases, but it does not break the page’s tonal consistency. The result feels editorial, not catalog-like.

The system prefers quiet borders, subtle shadow, and high alignment precision. Cards sit flush and evenly spaced. Text blocks are usually centered in the hero and the accessory row, then switch to left alignment in the spec table and the modal cards. This shift in alignment creates structure without extra decoration. The page also uses contrast between tight component clusters and large empty fields to signal importance. When a section matters, it gets space. When it supports the story, it gets a smaller footprint.

## Components

### Hero purchase rail

- **Anatomy:** Title, two pill tabs, color swatches, a pair of pricing cards, a deposit note, and a primary button.
- **Surface:** A pale card set against a broad light canvas.
- **Typography:** Centered heading with smaller body text inside the pricing module.
- **Shape:** Pills for tabs, rounded cards for pricing, and a fully rounded button.
- **Spacing:** Tight vertical stacking with clear breaks between tabs, swatches, pricing, and CTA.
- **Composition:** The rail is narrow and vertically centered, which keeps it secondary to the image.
- **Visible states:** Tabs read as selectable chips; the button reads as the one strong action on the page.

### Product hero image block

- **Anatomy:** Large robot render inside a rounded field.
- **Surface:** Soft off-white with almost no decoration.
- **Typography:** None inside the image block; the product itself becomes the focal point.
- **Shape:** Large rounded container with a clean internal crop.
- **Spacing:** The image gets the largest share of the viewport and should never feel boxed in.
- **Composition:** The robot sits slightly off center and stretches vertically, giving the page a sculptural feel.
- **Visible states:** The image block remains static and calm; it is not a gallery with competing controls.

### Accessory tile

- **Anatomy:** One centered object and one short caption.
- **Surface:** Pale cream panel with a subtle tonal variation from the canvas.
- **Typography:** Small centered caption with enough separation from the object.
- **Shape:** Large rounded rectangle with soft corners.
- **Spacing:** Even padding on all sides, with the object visually anchored in the middle of the card.
- **Composition:** Repetition across three cards creates a showroom row.
- **Visible states:** The tiles appear uniform, which keeps the section orderly and easy to scan.

### Hardware specification table

- **Anatomy:** Section label, grouped headings, left labels, right values, and faint separators.
- **Surface:** Plain white or near-white field with very light rules.
- **Typography:** Small, compact text with clear contrast between labels and values.
- **Shape:** No heavy card framing; the grid is the structure.
- **Spacing:** Large vertical gaps between groups and moderate padding within each row.
- **Composition:** Two-column alignment keeps the reading path predictable.
- **Visible states:** The table is informational rather than interactive; the design should stay calm and legible at high density.

### Utility modal

- **Anatomy:** Large dialog shell, small top navigation, centered title, two text cards, two large photo tiles, and a row of smaller cards.
- **Surface:** White dialog over a dimmed background.
- **Typography:** Centered modal title, then smaller card headings and body copy.
- **Shape:** Very rounded outer shell and rounded internal cards.
- **Spacing:** Strong padding around the dialog edge, then even gaps between internal blocks.
- **Composition:** The top cards explain the mode; the photo tiles show use; the smaller row finishes the narrative.
- **Visible states:** The dialog feels open and layered, but still controlled and tidy.

### FAQ modal

- **Anatomy:** Centered heading and a long stack of question rows.
- **Surface:** White dialog with a darkened scrim behind it.
- **Typography:** Bold question text inside each row.
- **Shape:** Each row is a rounded pill-like block inside the larger shell.
- **Spacing:** Vertical rhythm is the main organizing device; rows are separated by generous gutters.
- **Composition:** The stack is narrow and linear, which makes scanning easy.
- **Visible states:** Each row reads as an expandable item, even when the state is closed.

### Footer

- **Anatomy:** Tiny logo, grouped link columns, and faint legal text.
- **Surface:** Empty white field with no heavy banding.
- **Typography:** Small, quiet legal and navigation text.
- **Shape:** No major container, only spacing and alignment.
- **Spacing:** Link columns sit far apart, leaving a large central void.
- **Composition:** The footer feels like a coda, not a content dump.
- **Visible states:** Links are subdued; nothing in the footer should compete with the product or purchase path.

## Responsive behavior

The mobile screen condenses the hero into a vertical order stack. The title sits above the tabs, swatches, pricing cards, and CTA, all centered and compressed into one column. That pattern should become the default small-screen treatment for the purchase flow. The desktop rail should not be forced to remain narrow; it should instead stack naturally and preserve the reading order.

Accessory cards should collapse from three across to one or two per row, while keeping the object centered and the caption low in the tile. The hardware table should reduce to a simple one-column rhythm or short grouped rows, because dense two-column comparisons become hard to scan on small screens. The modal shell should widen to the viewport with careful side padding, but its internal cards should restack before text becomes cramped. The footer columns should also collapse into a short vertical list with the logo still separated from the links.

## Practical implementation guidance

### Preserve

- Keep the page almost monochrome. Use black for the primary action and dark text, then let pale neutrals do the rest.
- Preserve the large image-first hero. The robot render should remain the strongest object on the page.
- Keep the rounded panel language consistent across hero controls, accessory tiles, modal shells, and footer-adjacent blocks.
- Preserve the quiet typography. Most text should stay small, controlled, and tightly grouped.
- Keep the technical table sparse and disciplined so it still feels related to the hero.

### Avoid

- Avoid bright accent colors. They would break the calm hardware mood.
- Avoid heavy shadows, thick borders, and glossy effects.
- Avoid mixing many type families or using decorative display faces.
- Avoid turning every section into a card grid; the page needs large open fields as well as grouped modules.
- Avoid compressing the modal content so much that the photo tiles and question rows lose their breathing room.

### Recommended build order

1. Build the hero canvas and purchase rail.
2. Add the shared rounded-card and pill-button primitives.
3. Build the accessory row as a repeatable three-card pattern.
4. Add the hardware table with strict left/right alignment.
5. Add the modal shell and reuse it for utility and FAQ content.
6. Finish with the sparse footer and then tune the mobile stack.

### Accessibility

- Keep button text and card captions large enough to read against the pale surfaces.
- Make the primary CTA unmistakable through fill and contrast, not color alone.
- Preserve clear focus treatment on the tabs, button, and modal controls.
- Ensure that dense spec rows maintain readable spacing when text reflows.
- Give the robot image and the accessory objects meaningful alternative text that explains the product, not the styling.

## Scope note

This guide covers the NEO order page, the mobile purchase stack, the desktop hero, the accessory gallery, the hardware specification area, the utility and FAQ dialogs, and the footer. It does not define motion, hover choreography, loading states, alternate breakpoints, or authenticated purchase flows. Measurements are practical adaptation targets.
