## Overview

15th.plus-ex.com uses a strict monochrome language that feels closer to a poster series than a conventional site. The page relies on enormous Neuehaasgrotesk type, very open canvas, and a recurring contrast between white presentation stages and black editorial stages. The largest elements are not decorative containers but letterforms, numbers, and simple geometric marks: a plus sign, an X, diagonal slats, and a few rounded image cards.

The system never needs saturated color to establish hierarchy. White surfaces carry logo grids, award announcements, and big statement headings. Black surfaces carry the footer, project index, and story panels. Gray appears only as support: softer copy, divider lines, translucent beams, and the muted body of the index. That restraint gives the page a ceremonial tone and keeps attention on the typographic scale.

The strongest visual rule is contrast in scale. One section may contain a 200px-class numeral or a full-width headline, while the next uses tiny utility text and sparse contact details. The result is a page that feels expansive, editorial, and highly composed without becoming ornate.

## Colors

The color system is almost entirely achromatic. Black and white are the two active poles; the grays exist to keep the structure legible without pulling focus away from the type and logos.

| token | value | role |
|---|---|---|
| `ink` | `#000000` | Main text on white stages, dark-stage canvas, and the bold parts of the logo and award system |
| `canvas` | `#FFFFFF` | Page fields, logo walls, award panels, and the bright background used for the strongest typographic moments |
| `muted-ink` | `#4C4C4C` | Small footer lines, secondary labels, and lower-contrast utility text on dark surfaces |
| `soft-ink` | `#4D4D4D` | Close sibling to `muted-ink` for slightly warmer small text and subdued rows |
| `subdued-ink` | `#808080` | Dim index entries and quiet navigation-style labels |
| `border` | `#BBBBBB` | Thin rules, hairline outlines, and quiet structure on white stages |

On white stages, black text is the default voice and gray acts as a hush rather than a second brand color. On black stages, white text takes over for the main statement, while muted gray is reserved for the least important metadata. The page does not depend on gradients or color blocks to separate content. Instead, it uses shifts between black and white surfaces, then lets the grays do the work of restraint and hierarchy.

The palette is intentionally narrow so the typography and spacing carry the composition. Gray is never decorative here; it marks secondary information, translucent geometry, and the low-contrast rows that sit behind the highlighted item in the index. The border token is equally quiet and is best used as a fine separator or a chip outline, not as a heavy container edge. Any attempt to introduce a richer palette would fight the site’s disciplined poster mood.

## Typography

Neuehaasgrotesk carries the whole identity at the large end of the scale. Applesystem and Apple Sd Gothic Neo appear only in the smallest utility and metadata tiers. That split matters: the page feels emphatic and modern in its big headings, then unexpectedly quiet in the supporting text.

Licensing for Neuehaasgrotesk should be confirmed separately before reuse.

| token | family | size | weight | leading | tracking | use |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `hero-display` | Neuehaasgrotesk | 12.69rem | 700 | 0.8 | -0.03em | Monumental anniversary numerals and the largest page statements |
| `section-display` | Neuehaasgrotesk | 5.79rem | 700 | 0.88 | -0.02em | Large award headlines and oversized page callouts |
| `display-medium` | Neuehaasgrotesk | 2.062rem | 500 | 1 | -0.02em | Mid-page titles, brand marks, and card-scale headings |
| `display-small` | Neuehaasgrotesk | 1.983rem | 500 | 1.05 | 0em | Short emphatic labels and compact informational headings |
| `nav-link` | Neuehaasgrotesk | 1rem | 400 | 1 | 0.0625em | Thin utility navigation, footer links, and small directional text |
| `body` | Applesystem | 0.875rem | 400 | 1.4 | 0em | Contact details, footer lines, and other low-emphasis prose |
| `micro-label` | Apple Sd Gothic Neo | 0.625rem | 400 | 1.2 | 0em | Tiny support labels and the most discreet metadata |

The hierarchy works through size and density more than through variety. Display type is set tight and heavy, often with little more than a sliver of spacing between lines. Utility copy opens up and becomes lighter, especially on the dark footer and index sections where the page needs many small facts to sit quietly. Uppercase labels and short chips gain emphasis through spacing and outline rather than weight.

The visual system depends on the type family doing more than one job. At the largest scales, Neuehaasgrotesk becomes the identity mark itself; at medium scales, it behaves like a label family for project names and section headings; at small scales, it is still clean enough to carry navigation and list rows without collapsing into generic system text. The two smaller supporting families keep the page practical and calm, which prevents the oversized display faces from feeling theatrical.

## Layout

The page is organized as a sequence of full-width chapters, not as a single continuous grid. White chapters are spacious and centered. Black chapters are more editorial and left-anchored. The transition between them feels deliberate, almost like changing slides in a presentation.

The white stages are the most open. The logo wall distributes marks with enormous air between them, so the empty field becomes part of the composition. The awards pages do something similar, but with type and geometry instead of logos: a giant number or title occupies the center while diagonal gray beams and floating tiles create movement around it. These pages depend on broad negative space; any tighter packing would weaken the ceremonial tone.

The black stages have a different rhythm. They use long vertical breathing room, narrow gutters, and rows of quiet gray text. In the portfolio index, the right-hand column of years and categories is structurally clearer than the left, and one row receives brighter treatment so the eye can find the current item. In the footer and story panel, the layout pulls the eye to the left statement first, then lets the device image or social block finish the composition. The page feels broad rather than dense, even when it contains many names.

Spacing is consistently generous. The visual system depends on large section breaks, roomy outer margins, and a few smaller cluster gaps inside the chapter. The most important content is always given the most blank space around it.

A second layout rule is asymmetry. The design prefers left-weighted text blocks, central markers, and offset media rather than a perfectly centered brochure grid. That asymmetry gives the white chapters room to breathe and keeps the black chapters from becoming symmetrical dashboards. The repeated long gaps between chapters create a slow pace, so every transition feels like a new plate in the same series.

## Visual language

The recurring motif is the crossed mark: plus signs, X shapes, and diagonal bars. Those forms appear behind numbers, across award pages, and again in the footer as a minimal graphic signature. They turn a simple anniversary page into a system of intersections, which suits a brand that wants to look connected without becoming busy.

The page also uses a strict contrast between hard geometry and soft containment. Large text and diagonal beams are sharp and unapologetic. Small product cards and thumbnail images soften that energy with 12px corners and compact framing. The cards never become the main structure; they act as visual punctuation. On the white stages, this produces a gallery-like feeling. On the black stages, it produces a catalog-like feeling.

Lettering often carries more weight than imagery. The selected awards page is mostly about the scale of the words, while the product-list section is mostly about how the rows and years relate. The image cards matter, but they work best when they stay secondary to the composition of text, rules, and empty space. That is the key visual discipline of the site: let the typography and the simple geometric marks do the storytelling.

The visual system also treats rhythm as part of the language. A giant headline may sit beside a small chip or a low-contrast label, and that jump is not noise; it is the point. The page uses that change in density to signal ceremony, credibility, and motion. The black sections add weight by removing light, while the white sections add weight by removing clutter. In both cases, the composition stays sparse enough for each element to feel deliberate.

## Components

### Logo wall

- **Anatomy:** A grid of partner marks and wordmarks, each given its own open cell.
- **Surface:** White canvas, no boxes, no visible framing around most logos.
- **Typography:** Brand marks vary, but the system around them remains small and calm.
- **Composition:** The marks sit in a centered matrix with very large whitespace between rows and columns.
- **Role:** This component communicates reach and credibility through volume and spacing rather than through badges or testimonials.

### Awards hero

- **Anatomy:** Oversized year or number, thin gray diagonal bars, and a small outlined chip such as `AWARDS`.
- **Surface:** White field with black type and translucent gray geometry.
- **Typography:** The number uses the largest Neuehaasgrotesk scale in the system, while the chip uses tiny utility sizing.
- **Shape:** The chip is compact and rounded; the bars are long, flat, and intentionally blunt.
- **Composition:** The number is the anchor; the chip gives the page a point of entry without diluting the scale.
- **Visible states:** The highlight is created by contrast and placement, not by animation or color change.

### Selected awards headline panel

- **Anatomy:** Stacked uppercase headline fragments spread across the page.
- **Surface:** White background with a faint gray X-like structure behind the words.
- **Typography:** Heavy, compact Neuehaasgrotesk at a size that dominates the viewport.
- **Composition:** The words are split into blocks so the eye moves across them in steps rather than reading a single centered column.
- **Role:** This is the page’s loudest textual moment and should stay free of extra ornament.

### Portfolio index

- **Anatomy:** Category labels, project names, years, and a centrally placed showcase card.
- **Surface:** Black background with gray rows and one brighter selected row.
- **Typography:** Smaller Neuehaasgrotesk for list entries, with stronger white treatment on the highlighted item.
- **Composition:** Rows run horizontally with a strong left-right split; the center card interrupts the grid and gives the list a gallery quality.
- **Spacing:** Vertical separation matters more than border weight; the rows feel spaced apart rather than boxed.
- **Visible states:** The brighter row marks the current focus.

### Story panel and footer stage

- **Anatomy:** A large statement on one side, a device or card image on the other, followed by a compact footer with contact lines and social icons.
- **Surface:** Deep black with white text and muted gray support lines.
- **Typography:** The main statement uses the strongest white display type; the supporting details drop to small utility size.
- **Shape:** Rounded thumbnails and small icon buttons sit against otherwise straight-edged sections.
- **Composition:** The statement leads, the device image answers it, and the footer closes the page with a quiet utility block.
- **Role:** This is where the site becomes most editorial and least product-like.

The component set is small, but each part has a clear role in the page hierarchy. The logo wall establishes breadth, the awards hero establishes scale, the headline panel establishes force, the index establishes system logic, and the story/footer stage establishes closure. None of these parts rely on elaborate controls or decorative chrome. Their strength comes from arrangement, spacing, and the disciplined reuse of the same type family across different weights and sizes.

## Responsive behavior

The design depends on protecting the hierarchy when space tightens. The largest headlines must stay legible before anything else changes. The logo wall needs to keep its breathing room so the marks do not collapse into a dense strip. The black index needs to preserve its left-right reading order, because the category and year structure is part of the design language. Rounded cards should remain visibly separate from the background, and the small footer copy should keep enough contrast to stay readable. Any responsive version should keep the same order of emphasis: statement, supporting mark, then quiet metadata.

A responsive pass should reduce surface density before it reduces type scale. That means trimming lateral whitespace only after the main headings still read cleanly, keeping the black-and-white contrast intact, and preserving the long vertical pauses between sections. The page should never turn into a compact card grid. Instead, it should stay like a sequence of plates, only allowing the interior spacing to compress when the viewport truly forces it.

## Practical implementation guidance

### Preserve

- Keep the page monochrome. The system gets its identity from black, white, and restrained gray, not from accent color.
- Preserve the scale jumps. The jump from giant display type to tiny utility text is the main visual event.
- Keep large surfaces empty around the main mark, logo grid, or award headline.
- Retain the small rounded chips and image cards, but let them stay secondary.
- Use thin rules and muted text instead of heavy separators.

### Avoid

- Avoid decorative gradients, glow, or soft shadows.
- Avoid colorful badges, card borders, or dashboard-like containers.
- Avoid compressing the logo wall or award pages into a dense marketing grid.
- Avoid mixing in a second display family.
- Avoid turning the footer or index into a standard SaaS navigation block.

### Recommended build order

1. Set the black-and-white palette and the main Neuehaasgrotesk scale.
2. Rebuild the white logo wall with its sparse grid logic.
3. Recreate the award pages with the oversized number and diagonal beam motif.
4. Add the dark index with its two-column reading rhythm.
5. Finish with the story panel and footer stage, keeping utility copy small and quiet.

### Accessibility

- Keep white text on black surfaces and black text on white surfaces at strong contrast.
- Do not let muted gray become the only cue for important links or active rows.
- Provide clear alternative text for partner marks, award cards, and device images.
- Keep the outlined chip and social icons reachable and visibly distinct from the background.
- Make sure the smallest utility text remains readable at the final rendered size.

The implementation should favor simple containers, explicit spacing tokens, and a small set of reusable type styles. That keeps the visual language intact when the layout is rebuilt in code. Any component meant to hold a logo, chip, row, or card should use the same spacing logic and border softness so the site remains coherent. The important part is not the raw component count; it is whether each surface still feels like it belongs to the same anniversary system.

## Scope note

This guide covers the black-and-white home and index surfaces on 15th.plus-ex.com: the logo wall, award chapters, dark portfolio rows, story panel, and footer. It does not include mobile-specific layouts, motion rules, hover or focus states, or complete font licensing details. Type and spacing values follow the site’s 0.125rem step.
