## Overview

1600.agency is built like a refined sales page for a motion studio: bright, centered, and unusually calm for a service site that sells speed and output. The page leans on a white canvas, a heavy black display face, and one warm orange action color. That combination makes the hero feel crisp and confident rather than decorative. The interface never becomes busy; the visual message is always easy to scan because the strongest elements are held in a small number of large, rounded containers.

The page story moves in clear chapters. A pill-shaped header introduces the brand and the main appointment action. The hero follows with a large claim, short support copy, compact badges, and a strong CTA. Proof appears as a gray logo row and testimonial cards, then the page shifts into a pale process grid and a dark pricing stage. The footer closes the experience in near-black, with city times, contact details, and compact links. The whole system depends on contrast between white openness and dark terminal sections.

## Colors

The palette is intentionally small. White is the main field, not a background accent, and the black family is used for typography, the header rail, the primary appointment button, and the pricing/footer bookends. The orange action color carries the page’s energy. It marks the active offer, the badge accents, the progress labels, and the secondary highlights inside the pricing section. It should stay warm and saturated, not drift into a pink or red brand note.

The grays do the quiet structural work. `#E7E7E7` and `#EDEDED` shape borders, pill outlines, card edges, and the pale review/process surfaces. `#A9A9A9` and `#525252` handle supporting copy and secondary metadata. The overall effect is a page that reads almost like a monochrome layout until the orange details step in. Green is reserved for the chat/contact action; it should stay isolated so it keeps its instant-recognition role. The dark sections are not simply inverted light sections: `#171717` and `#101010` create a dense stage that makes the white pricing matrix and pale logos feel brighter without adding extra decoration.

## Typography

Visby Cf is the voice of the page. It carries the hero, section heads, pricing titles, and other large statements with very compact leading and a heavy, rounded look. Dm Sans 9 Pt supports the longer selling copy and the explanatory lines beneath the hero and cards. Dm Sans handles compact controls, badges, and smaller labels. Plus Jakarta Sans appears in the quieter footer and legal lines. Inter shows up in small utility text in the packet and fits the same restrained role. Font licensing is not included in the packet and should be confirmed before reuse.

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|

| section-display | Visby Cf | 2rem | 700 | 1.05 | -0.01em | Section titles and short leads |

The hierarchy depends more on scale and weight than on stylistic variation. Display text is dense and black, but the supporting copy is never tiny; it remains readable at 16–20px and uses breathing room instead of extra ornament. Tracking is tight in the hero and labels, which helps the page feel engineered rather than playful.

## Layout

The layout is centered and symmetrical at the top, then becomes more sectional farther down the page. The header is a long white pill with ample internal padding, rounded ends, and a strong left-to-right order: mark, navigation, language selector, action button, chat button. The hero continues that centered logic. A large headline sits above a short paragraph, then a row of compact chips, then the black CTA, then a trust line and logo row. This stack gives the page a strong vertical spine.

Spacing is generous and repetitive rather than complex. The page uses wide gutters, large clearances between chapters, and substantial insets inside the major cards. The hero sits far enough below the header that the title can breathe. The process grid and testimonial area use roomy pale cards with wide interior padding so the page never feels crowded. The pricing stage is the strongest layout break: a dark container with an inset white pricing matrix and a separate presentation preview creates a decisive switch in tone. The footer repeats the same discipline in reverse, lowering contrast and tightening the type as the page reaches utility information.

Rounded corners are a key spatial cue. The shell uses very broad radii, the main cards use medium radii, and the chips stay pill-like. That combination keeps the interface soft without turning it into a generic bubbly template.

## Visual language

The visual language mixes corporate clarity with studio polish. The top area feels like a premium service landing page: clean white field, bold black claim, orange check chips, and a restrained CTA. The green chat button is a small, highly legible exception that adds immediate contact energy without competing with the main appointment action. The page also uses a few subtle product-like details — thin gray outlines, soft chip fills, and a muted video stage — to keep the composition tactile.

Proof is presented with the least possible friction. Client logos sit in a low-contrast strip and function as trust markers, not as a busy gallery. Testimonial cards are white, airy, and text-led, with small quote marks and small avatars. The process section turns into four pale blocks with orange stage labels and embedded screenshots, which makes the delivery method feel concrete rather than abstract. The pricing stage goes bolder: dark framing, a bright orange active choice, white option cards, and black utility tiles. That black-and-white split gives the pricing area the most contrast on the page and makes it feel like the decisive end of the sales story.

Shadows are light and selective. The page does not rely on deep elevation stacks. Instead, separation comes from white space, card borders, and surface changes. That restraint is part of the brand.

## Components

### Header rail

- **Anatomy:** logo mark, four text links, language selector, primary appointment button, green chat control.
- **Surface:** white pill on white canvas, with a soft gray edge rather than a hard frame.
- **Typography:** small bold labels and compact utility text.
- **Shape:** the outer rail is very round; internal controls keep their own pill geometry.
- **Spacing:** horizontal rhythm is loose enough that the links never crowd the action cluster.
- **Composition:** left content carries navigation; right content carries action and support.

### Hero stack

- **Anatomy:** oversized headline, short supporting sentence, orange check chips, black CTA, trust line, logo row.
- **Surface:** plain white, with no decorative frame around the text.
- **Typography:** the headline is the dominant element; the support copy stays medium-sized and centered.
- **Shape:** chips and buttons are pill-based; the hero itself stays open and rectangular.
- **Hierarchy:** the CTA sits under the badges, not beside them, which keeps the reading path vertical and deliberate.

### Trust row and proof blocks

- **Anatomy:** gray brand names, light testimonial cards, small avatar circles, quote marks, role lines.
- **Surface:** white for cards, very light gray for supporting containers.
- **Typography:** text remains easy to scan and uses quiet gray for roles and metadata.
- **Composition:** logos run in a horizontal row; testimonials are arranged as separate cards with a calm grid rhythm.
- **Visible state:** the cards look resting and editorial, not interactive or tabbed.

### Process cards

- **Anatomy:** orange week label, black card title, explanatory sentence, miniature screen or sticker composition.
- **Surface:** pale gray or near-white cards with thin borders.
- **Typography:** titles are bold and compact; body text is centered and slightly softer.
- **Shape:** large rounded rectangles hold each week.
- **Spacing:** the cards keep a lot of interior air so the screenshots can sit low in the frame without feeling cramped.
- **Hierarchy:** the week label is secondary in size but primary in color, which keeps the sequence legible.

### Pricing stage

- **Anatomy:** black outer stage, preview panel, white offer matrix, orange price accents, selectable tiles.
- **Surface:** the outer container is near-black; the right-hand offer area is white and bright.
- **Typography:** pricing headlines are large and confident; option tiles use heavier labels and smaller amounts.
- **Shape:** both sides use generous radii, but the black stage feels heavier because of its color block.
- **Composition:** the layout splits into a dark explanation side and a bright choice side, which makes the offer feel concrete and direct.
- **State language:** one option is filled orange to show the current choice, while the rest stay white.

### Footer

- **Anatomy:** contact block, city/time columns, navigation row, social mark, legal line.
- **Surface:** deep black or near-black, with low-contrast text.
- **Typography:** small, calm, and compact; the hierarchy drops from city headings to fine legal lines.
- **Composition:** the footer spreads laterally across columns, then compresses into a narrow legal strip.
- **Mood:** it feels like the closing room after the main pitch, not like a second hero.

## Responsive behavior

The desktop page depends on centered stacking, wide gutters, and multi-column cards. On narrower screens, the same hierarchy should stay intact: logo and actions first, headline and chips next, then trust, proof, process, pricing, and footer. The design can tolerate a tighter width only if the pills keep their breathing room and the dark pricing stage still reads as the end-point. Keep the large headline from collapsing into a cramped block; the identity relies on that strong opening scale.

## Practical implementation guidance

### Preserve

- Keep the page white-first and reserve black for the header CTA, pricing stage, and footer.
- Preserve the orange action color as the one warm accent that carries emphasis across the page.
- Keep the pill header, rounded cards, and large panel corners consistent across sections.
- Maintain the centered hero stack and the trust row directly beneath it.
- Let proof feel calm and readable; the site should sell through clarity, not density.

### Avoid

- Avoid thin enterprise-style cards with square corners.
- Avoid heavy shadows on every component; the page works through spacing and contrast.
- Avoid overfilling the hero with extra badges, icons, or secondary calls to action.
- Avoid mixing too many font families in one block; the page depends on a disciplined hierarchy.

### Recommended build order

1. Build the header rail and its rounded action cluster.
2. Set the hero stack with Visby Cf and the orange chip row.
3. Add the trust logo strip and testimonial cards.
4. Build the process grid with pale cards and embedded screenshots.
5. Add the pricing stage with the split dark/white composition.
6. Finish with the footer columns and legal row.

### Accessibility

- Keep the black CTA and the orange active pricing tile at strong contrast against their surroundings.
- Use clear alternative text for all portraits, screenshots, and logo marks.
- Make sure the green chat button is not the only contact path.
- Preserve visible focus states on the header links, CTA button, chips, and pricing options.
- Keep the smaller gray footer copy readable and do not let it drop below comfortable contrast.

## Scope note

This guide covers the desktop homepage surfaces shown here: header, hero, trust row, testimonial cards, process grid, pricing stage, and footer. It does not define mobile stacking, hover or focus styling, animation, video playback behavior, or unshown interior pages. Spacing and radii follow the packet’s 2px step and are expressed as rounded rem values.
