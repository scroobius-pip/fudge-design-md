## Design character

10er is a dark, product-marketing surface for fan-funded creator payments. The system is **high-contrast monochrome SaaS**: near-black canvas, soft zinc grays for secondary text, and white as the only hard accent on primary actions. Layouts are centered and sparse — one big claim, a short proof line, then a single path to act. The vibe is calm confidence rather than flashy fintech.

What should survive adaptation:
- A **true dark canvas** with light gray body text, not muddy mid-grays.
- **Huge display numbers and headlines** that own the viewport before any UI chrome.
- **Pill CTAs and chips** as the main interactive language.
- Pricing as a clean comparison of a few solid cards, not a dense table.

## Foundations

### Color

Measured roles and surfaces:
- Primary text on dark: `#f4f4f5`
- Secondary / muted text: `#9f9fa9`
- Accent / inverse controls: `#ffffff` and `#000000` depending on local surface
- Canvas reads near-black; cards lift slightly above the page

The palette is intentionally short. There is no bright brand hue in the chrome — contrast and weight do the work. White buttons on black and black type on white pricing cards create the only strong figure/ground flips.

### Typography

Declared family names were not captured; the face reads as a modern geometric sans.
Observed scale:
- Hero display: **96px / 96px**, weight 700 — tight line-height for big pricing numerals and claims
- Section titles: **24px / 32px**, weight 700
- Card titles: **20px / 28px**, weight 600
- Body and nav: **16px / 24–26px**, weights 400–500
- Meta / fine print: **14px / 20px**, weight 500

Hierarchy is size-and-weight first. Tracking is quiet; the display sizes carry the drama.

### Spacing and layout

Recurring rhythm:
- Section padding around **64px** vertical
- Horizontal content inset that keeps a centered column (large side margins on wide pricing views)
- Control padding **8–12px**; card internal gaps **16–24px**
- Home feels airy; pricing stacks three equal plan cards in one row under a centered headline

Content width is deliberately constrained so the giant type never feels lost edge-to-edge.

## Visual language

- **Pills everywhere**: primary buttons, secondary chips, and avatar/status clusters use full-round or large radii.
- Cards on pricing use soft rounded rectangles (~**24px** radius) with 1px solid borders in low-contrast gray.
- Shadows are minimal; depth comes from value steps (black → charcoal card → white control).
- Imagery is sparse on marketing frames — product UI mock or simple icons rather than photography-led heroes.
- Motion is not established from still frames; treat transitions as optional.

## Components and states

Recurring pieces:
1. **Top nav** — logo left, text links, small ghost/secondary actions, solid white primary on the right.
2. **Hero stack** — social proof, massive headline, one-line subcopy, primary + secondary CTA row.
3. **Pricing cards** — plan name, giant price, short feature list, full-width CTA; one plan may be emphasized with inverted or bordered treatment.
4. **Feature rows** — icon or short label + concise body at 16px.
5. **Footer** — quiet multi-column links on the same dark ground.

Visible states are mostly default and emphasis (filled vs outline). Do not invent hover/focus specifics beyond the clear default/emphasis split.

## Responsive behavior

Captures span roughly 1689–1712px widths with both short and tall viewports. The system stays single-column hero + multi-card pricing at desktop widths. Mobile collapse is not directly observed here — when adapting, keep the centered column and let cards stack before shrinking type too aggressively.

## Practical guidance

**Preserve**
- Black field, zinc text, white primary action
- Oversized display type for money and claims
- Pill geometry on interactive controls
- Few colors; high contrast

**Avoid**
- Adding a loud brand rainbow to the chrome
- Dense enterprise tables on pricing
- Light-mode defaults without redesigning contrast
- Soft gray-on-gray body copy that kills the crisp monochrome read
