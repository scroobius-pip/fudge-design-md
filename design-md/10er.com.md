## Design character

10er presents creator funding as friendly, direct, and visually confident. Its foundation is a deep charcoal field, but the identity is not purely monochrome: the primary action is a vivid blue, the pricing label introduces a warm orange, and the home page is energized by a row of colorful podcast and project covers. The result feels like a focused creator platform rather than a conventional financial dashboard.

The design repeatedly moves between two modes:

- **Centered persuasion:** a large claim, a compact explanation, one blue action, then proof.
- **Quiet utility:** short navigation, plainly stated pricing, FAQs, and a low-contrast footer.

The important balance is expressive content inside a disciplined shell. Let creator artwork supply variety; keep the surrounding chrome dark, spacious, and typographically simple.

## Foundations

### Color

The captured palette supports these practical relationships:

- `#161618` — the near-black home and pricing canvas.
- `#f4f4f5` — high-contrast headings and primary copy on the dark field.
- `#a0a0aa` — supporting copy, explanatory text, and quieter labels.
- `#2b3034` — the lighter footer surface, which separates the closing navigation without becoming a promotional panel.
- `#3158d4` — the blue filled action used for “Get started” and “Get Started”.
- `#f1a25c` — the small warm pricing accent.

The blue should be reserved for the clearest next step, not spread across ordinary links. The orange works as a small signpost rather than a second button color. The home artwork can be saturated and multicolored because it is content, not chrome.

### Typography

A bold, rounded sans-serif hierarchy carries the interface. Family identification is not available in the captured data, so implementations should match the proportions and weight rather than claim a specific licensed face.

- **Hero claim:** heavy, compact, and centered; it breaks into two lines in the wide home capture.
- **Key number:** the pricing percentage is the dominant element and should be treated almost like a graphic mark.
- **Navigation and actions:** medium-to-bold sans text keeps small controls legible against the charcoal field.
- **Supporting copy:** lighter gray and materially smaller than the headline, with generous line spacing.
- **Section labels:** short, bold labels such as “Pricing” create orientation without adding a heavy panel.

Use weight and contrast before introducing extra type sizes. The visual hierarchy is intentionally blunt: headline, action, proof, then detail.

### Spacing and layout

The home hero is a centered vertical stack with substantial empty space above and below the claim. The artwork row is allowed to become the visual base of the hero, while the search field sits underneath as a practical continuation. On pricing, the content column narrows and becomes more editorial: the percentage and action sit above a two-column explanation, followed by a wide FAQ list.

Useful layout rules:

- Keep the principal claim in a constrained measure; do not let it become a full-width banner.
- Give the hero enough vertical air for the artwork to read as a gallery, not a background texture.
- Use a two-column split for short pricing benefits and their explanation at wide widths.
- Separate the FAQ from the pricing statement with a clear vertical interval, not a card container.
- Use thin rules to organize FAQ rows and low-contrast footer columns.

Exact responsive breakpoints are not supported by the captures. Preserve the order—navigation, claim, action, proof/artwork, then search or detail—and allow the artwork row, pricing split, and footer columns to stack as the viewport narrows.

## Visual language

### Dark shell, vivid content

The shell is nearly black and deliberately untextured. Color enters through the blue CTA, the orange pricing marker, the yellow circular logo, and the creator covers. This makes every saturated element legible as a point of attention. Do not dilute the effect with a colorful navigation or decorative gradients.

### Tilted project covers

The home capture uses six large cover tiles arranged in a loose horizontal fan. Each tile has its own artwork and a slight rotation; neighboring tiles overlap or nearly touch, creating a tactile shelf of projects. The rotation is part of the composition, not an instruction to rotate every card in the product. Keep the individual cover artwork rectangular, let the group carry the playfulness, and anchor it inside the otherwise centered layout.

### Flat depth

Depth is created by contrast, borders, and separation rather than large shadows. The pricing page keeps the dark field open, uses horizontal rules for FAQ rows, and reserves the lighter charcoal value for the footer. Buttons are filled capsules; secondary controls are outlined capsules.

## Recurring components

### Header

Place the circular yellow 10 logo at the left. On wide layouts, keep “Creators,” “Features,” and “Pricing” as compact links toward the right, followed by an outlined “Find a project,” blue “Get started,” and a circular account control. The header is quiet and compact so the hero claim remains the first major read.

### Hero claim

Use a centered two-line statement with a short two-line explanation beneath it. The blue “Get started →” capsule is the primary conversion moment. Below it, a concise reassurance line and a small “300+” proof marker establish trust without adding a testimonial block.

### Project gallery and search

Treat the cover row as the home page’s expressive centerpiece. Maintain clear silhouettes and varied artwork, but keep the row on the dark ground. Follow it with a wide, low-contrast outlined search field labeled “Find a project...” so discovery reads as the next natural action.

### Pricing statement

Start with the small orange “Pricing” label, then the oversized “8%” figure and the underlined “+ transaction fees” line. The blue action sits immediately below. The next section pairs bold benefits (“No fixed costs,” “Direct payouts”) with a concise explanatory paragraph. Keep the copy visible and plain; do not turn it into a dense pricing matrix.

### FAQ

Use a narrow list of full-width rows. Each row has a bold question, a right-aligned chevron, and a thin divider. An expanded answer uses muted copy and a blue underlined external link. The open state is demonstrated; collapsed rows should retain the same row height and alignment.

### Footer

End on the lighter charcoal surface. Keep the logo, social marks, and short project note at left; organize links into compact groups such as Product, Discover, Tools, Legal, and Contact. Heading labels are muted, while links are bright enough to scan.

## Practical guidance

**Do:**

- Start with the dark shell and establish the blue action before adding supporting color.
- Use very large type for the central promise or price.
- Make creator/project artwork the source of visual variety.
- Prefer capsules, thin rules, and open space to heavy cards and shadows.
- Keep copy short, concrete, and close to the action it explains.

**Avoid:**

- Replacing the project gallery with generic finance imagery.
- Making every link blue or every surface colorful.
- Turning the pricing story into a dense table when a figure and two benefits communicate it more clearly.
- Adding responsive behavior, motion, or font licensing claims that are not established by the captured frames.

## Scope

This guide is grounded in the captured home and pricing views, including wide and compact-height frames. Exact mobile behavior, interaction motion, and the licensed names of the typefaces are not established here.
