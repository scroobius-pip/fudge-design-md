## Overview

Crazy Creative is a poster-like studio site built around one decisive move: a saturated pink field carrying enormous white type and a playful cartoon language. The first screen reads like a billboard rather than a conventional agency homepage. A compact top bar, a handwritten “wow,” a single giant claim, and a pill CTA sit above a mascot that peeks into frame from below. The composition is intentionally theatrical, but the structure is simple: one center stack, one action, one dominant illustration, and very little chrome.

The rest of the page keeps that same confidence but changes the surface. White pricing cards replace the pink field, then a dark illustrated band takes over, then a white FAQ block, and finally a black closing card sits inside another pink flame shape. The page depends on contrast jumps, rounded forms, and illustration more than on fine-grained layout detail. It feels loud, direct, and handcrafted.

## Colors

The color system is narrow in UI terms and wide in accent terms. The core interface is hot pink, white, black, and near-black. That is enough to carry the page because the headline, buttons, and cards do not need complicated color logic. The pink field is the brand’s main canvas and stays fully saturated. White is the universal foreground for the hero, the CTA pills, and the card surfaces. Near-black and black handle the secondary backplates, headings, and long-form text on the white sections.

The bright secondary colors belong mostly to illustration and ornament. Cyan, lime, purple, yellow, and gold appear as confetti, creature details, lightning-like shapes, and the little script accents that make the page feel handmade. They should stay away from routine text and controls so the interface does not collapse into noise. That separation matters: the page is expressive, but the reading system remains simple.

Light and dark modes are not symmetric. White sections feel like a reset between louder scenes. Dark sections are rarer and more dramatic; they work best as stage sets for illustration and a short, centered statement. On those dark bands, white text gains most of the contrast. On pink, the page uses white text and white cards to keep the whole composition bright rather than muddy. The design never depends on muted neutrals to soften the palette; it uses shape, scale, and framing instead.

## Typography

Crazy Creative uses two material families: **Bricolage Grotesque 96 Pt** for the oversized poster statements and **Bricolage Grotesque** for the supporting sentence level. The smaller utility chrome falls back to **System**. The whole hierarchy is a scale story, not a family story. The display face becomes the brand because it is pushed to extreme size with tight leading and a slightly compressed horizontal feel. Supporting copy stays large enough to feel deliberate, but lighter in impact.

Font licensing is not included in the packet; confirm rights before reuse.

| token | family | size | weight | leading | tracking | use |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| hero-display | Bricolage Grotesque 96 Pt | 8.125rem | 400 | 0.85 | -0.017em | Main hero claim on pink |
| section-display | Bricolage Grotesque 96 Pt | 8.125rem | 400 | 0.85 | -0.017em | Large chapter heading on light or dark stages |
| supporting-copy | Bricolage Grotesque | 1.75rem | 400 | 1.4 | -0.03em | Hero subcopy and large explanatory lines |
| card-heading | Bricolage Grotesque 96 Pt | 1.125rem | 700 | 1.4 | -0.017em | Plan labels, strong callout text, short bold labels |
| button-label | Bricolage Grotesque 96 Pt | 1.125rem | 700 | 1.4 | -0.017em | Pill CTAs and the top-right action |
| micro-label | System | 0.75rem | 400 | 1.2 | 0em | Side rail text, tiny chrome, form microcopy |

The headline style works because the face is large but not decorative. The script “wow” sits outside the system as an accent, not as a core text role. That keeps the page from needing extra font families to feel expressive. Use the display face for the emotional statement, the 28px family for the descriptive line, and the 12px system scale for tiny utility text. Do not upsize the utility chrome to compete with the main poster line.

## Layout

The page is organized as a sequence of posters. The hero is the clearest example: a wide pink field with the identity mark at upper left, navigation centered high across the top, a rounded action at upper right, and a vertical award-style rail pinned to the right edge. The message stack is centered and narrow, which makes the headline feel monumental even though the layout itself is simple. The mascot and confetti cut into the lower edge so the composition feels alive without needing a complex background treatment.

The next visible chapter changes format rather than just content. The pricing area becomes a white canvas with a huge black heading and two cards laid side by side. Each card is roomy, softly rounded, and separated by clean white space. The cards keep the reading order stable: small price line, larger offer statement, timeframe, benefits, and one pill CTA. The left-right balance is calm after the hero’s intensity.

The dark illustration band is the page’s most theatrical section after the hero. It uses a black or deep charcoal surface with a centered creature illustration and mirrored shapes spreading outward. The section’s meaning comes from the symmetry: the page goes from broad pink openness to a controlled, stage-like center. A rounded white arc at the bottom lightens the transition into the next section.

The FAQ area returns to white and uses a two-column structure. One side carries a large question header and a form, while the other side stacks question rows with clear separators. The closing section then switches back to pink and adds a black card framed by a flame-like wave. That final card gives the page a second CTA moment and closes the story with the same bold contrast as the opening screen.

Spacing stays generous throughout. The site uses wide side margins, strong vertical breathing room, and large rounded corners rather than dense grids. The visual pacing depends on those open fields as much as on the content inside them.

## Visual language

Crazy Creative uses flat color blocks, sticker-like illustration, and oversized type to make the site feel like a campaign poster. The little “wow” script and the mascot drawings add personality without turning the interface into a toy store. They sit on top of a very disciplined structure: a centered headline, a small action, a simple card, a dark band, a FAQ grid, then a final CTA. That contrast between wild imagery and stable scaffolding is the defining move.

The logo behaves like a graphic object, not a plain wordmark. It has a layered outline feel and lives comfortably inside the pink field. Rounded buttons echo that softness, while the cards and stage panels keep their own broad corner radius. Sharp corners are rare and only show up in small utility details. The overall effect is plush, thick, and visibly hand-finished.

Illustration does architectural work here. The mascot peeking from the hero, the mirrored creatures in the dark section, and the unicorn-and-flame closing scene each give their section a distinct identity. These are not separate decorations pasted on top of a neutral UI; they are the section headers in visual form. Keep that hierarchy intact. If the illustration is removed, the page loses its rhythm.

Color accents behave similarly. Cyan, lime, purple, and yellow should stay concentrated in drawings and special shapes so the controls remain legible. The UI itself should stay almost embarrassingly simple: white, black, and pink do most of the work. That restraint is what lets the wild parts feel intentional instead of chaotic.

## Components

### Header and edge chrome

- **Anatomy:** logo at upper left, three small navigation links centered high on the page, one rounded action at upper right, and a vertical edge rail on the right.
- **Surface:** transparent over the hero field, so the pink stage reads uninterrupted.
- **Typography:** compact, small-scale, and lighter than the hero statement. The header should never rival the main claim.
- **Shape:** the action is a pill; the rail is a narrow white rectangle with stacked text.
- **Visible state:** the right-edge rail reads like a badge, not a button, and stays visually separate from the main CTA.

### Hero stage

- **Anatomy:** handwritten accent, giant white headline, supporting sentence, pill button, and a mascot peeking into the lower frame.
- **Surface:** flat hot pink with no texture.
- **Typography:** display type is the entire show; the supporting line stays large enough to feel poster-like.
- **Composition:** centered stack with generous empty space around it; the bottom edge is intentionally broken by illustration.
- **Hierarchy:** the hero CTA is the only action in the first view that needs to feel primary.

### Pill buttons

- **Anatomy:** rounded pill, bold short label, clean center alignment.
- **Fill:** usually white on pink; the closing CTA can invert into a black card with a brighter lime button.
- **Typography:** bold 18px label, visually compact, with no extra icon needed.
- **Shape:** full pill radius, soft enough to look friendly but not blurry.
- **Spacing:** the label sits inside a roomy horizontal cushion, with enough top and bottom padding to feel tactile.

### Pricing cards

- **Anatomy:** price line, plan name, timeframe, short benefit list, full-width CTA.
- **Surface:** white cards on a white section, differentiated by borders, depth, and spacing rather than color.
- **Typography:** large black headline above, then smaller supporting copy and compact bullets inside each card.
- **Shape:** broad rounded corners with a stable rectangle body.
- **Visible state:** the cards feel parallel, not competitive; the page is presenting options, not forcing a comparison chart.

### Dark illustration stage

- **Anatomy:** centered headline, mirrored illustration, starry or spark-like accents, broad black field.
- **Surface:** near-black or black with bright illustration colors.
- **Typography:** white display type with very high contrast.
- **Composition:** symmetrical and theatrical; the illustration spans left and right so the center feels anchored.
- **Visible state:** this section should feel like a scene change, not a content block.

### FAQ and contact block

- **Anatomy:** oversized question headline, form inputs, small submit button, and a stacked list of common questions.
- **Surface:** white canvas with light separators and open spacing.
- **Typography:** the main question is large and black; the list items are smaller and calmer.
- **Composition:** two-column on desktop, with the form on one side and the question list on the other.
- **Visible state:** the row separators should be light and quiet, so the page still feels airy.

### Closing CTA card

- **Anatomy:** black rounded card, short logo, big closing line, and one bright CTA.
- **Surface:** the card sits inside a pink flame-like shape and becomes the final dark anchor on the page.
- **Typography:** white text on black, with the same strong display attitude as the hero.
- **Composition:** the card is large, centered, and deliberately simple so the flame shape stays legible.
- **Visible state:** the lime button should read as the final action, not a secondary note.

## Responsive behavior

On narrower screens, the page should keep the same order of emphasis: logo and small chrome first, headline second, CTA third, then illustration and section content. The hero must remain a poster stack rather than turning into a crowded multi-column layout. The pricing cards can collapse one above the other, but they should keep their generous padding and rounded corners. The FAQ can shift from two columns to one without losing the sense of open spacing. The page should preserve the edge-badge idea and the large illustration moments even when the crop becomes tighter.

The important responsive rule is not to reduce the scale too early. This design depends on oversized type and large illustrated shapes. If the hero headline becomes timid, the page loses its identity. Instead, keep the headline large, let the illustration crop more aggressively, and allow the spacing to compress only after the main poster shape is still clear.

## Practical implementation guidance

### Preserve

- Keep the first screen as a full-bleed pink poster with a narrow centered text stack.
- Keep the white pill CTA and the black/white contrast language consistent across sections.
- Keep the illustrated mascot, mirrored creatures, and closing flame card as section-defining shapes.
- Keep corners broad and soft; the 40px-style panel radius is part of the brand.
- Keep the headline oversized even when the viewport gets smaller.

### Avoid

- Avoid adding extra neutral grays to “soften” the palette.
- Avoid replacing illustration with photography or stock imagery.
- Avoid shrinking the headline into ordinary marketing copy.
- Avoid small enterprise-style cards with sharp corners.
- Avoid adding a second competing action color to the UI.

### Recommended build order

1. Build the hero stage, logo placement, and top chrome.
2. Add the pill button pattern and use it consistently.
3. Recreate the pricing cards on a white section.
4. Add the dark illustrated band with centered display text.
5. Build the FAQ form and question list.
6. Finish with the closing black card and pink flame frame.
7. Tune spacing and crops so the illustration stays part of the composition.

### Accessibility

- Use white text on pink and white text on black only where the contrast stays strong at the final crop.
- Give the mascot, unicorn, and other decorative illustration meaningful alt text only when they carry content; otherwise keep them decorative.
- Keep focus rings visible on the pill CTAs, form inputs, and any row controls.
- Make sure the FAQ rows are keyboard-accessible and clearly separated.
- Do not rely on color alone to distinguish the CTA states; shape and placement should make them obvious.

## Scope note

This guide covers the homepage hero, pricing cards, dark illustrated pricing band, FAQ/contact section, and closing CTA on crazycreative.design. Spacing and size values use the packet’s 0.125rem step. It does not define motion, hover or loading states, alternate breakpoints, or broader page templates.
